# LD-06 — Admin metrics and telemetry for research and improvement: purpose, anonymisation, cell size, retention

Scope: synthetic and test use only (owner decision D25, 3 Oct 2026). A licensed Turkish lawyer countersigns before any real patient.

## Question

The owner asks for admin metrics and telemetry about users and questions, for research and enhancement, as far as the law allows. In scope: aggregate, de-identified metrics per question and protocol version — completion and drop-off, time-to-answer, repeat and clarify rate, typed versus voice share, ASR quality bands, the share of sessions reaching RED, YELLOW or GREEN, device and locale — and user funnels invited, started, finished, shared; small cells suppressed; no free text, no identifiers, no per-patient drill-down. (a) Do improvement analytics fall under the existing service purpose? (b) Is research use a separate purpose that needs explicit consent? (c) Anonymised or pseudonymised: what may the admin analytics hold? (d) What minimum cell size must a count reach before it shows? (e) How long may the data be kept?

## Decision

(a) Improving the service is a purpose of its own, not the patient's care, so it does not ride the care purpose (KVKK m.4(2)(c)-(ç)). It needs no ground and no consent of its own as long as only anonymised aggregates come out: the Law does not apply to personal data processed for research, planning and statistics by being anonymised (m.28(1)(b)), and anonymised data cannot be associated with an identified or identifiable person even when matched with other data (m.3(1)(b)). Anonymisation is the condition: a figure that is not anonymous loses this route. In the hospital deployment the vendor processes on the hospital's authority and on its behalf (m.3(1)(ğ)), so producing aggregates for the vendor's own improvement needs the hospital's authorisation in the DPA; the hospital itself may analyse its own service for planning and management under m.6(3)(e). (b) Research on anonymised aggregates follows the same route (m.28(1)(b)); the Ministry's own regulation allows scientific work on health data the controller has anonymised (KSVY m.16(1)). Research on record-level or pseudonymised data is a separate purpose (m.4(2)(c)) on special-category data, and the product's ground for it is the patient's açık rıza under the existing research purpose (m.6(3)(a)); whether m.28(1)(c) and KSVY m.16(2) offer another route is HB-L26 and stays with counsel. None of it reaches the admin panel. (c) The admin analytics hold anonymised aggregates only. Anonymisation means no one can be identified even by reversal or by matching with other data, by the controller or any recipient (Yönetmelik m.10(1)-(2)); a keyed or hashed per-person or per-session row can be reversed by whoever holds the key, so pseudonymised rows remain personal data — health data — and are never admin analytics. Following the Kurul's guide, indirect identifiers count too, and every combination of dimensions the panel can show is a cell of its own. (d) No retrieved text sets a number. The legal test is the outcome in Yönetmelik m.10(2); the Kurul's guide names k-anonymity as a method that strengthens anonymisation without fixing a k. The product floor of ten distinct people per cell (files/06 §8; the INT-171 gate), counted per person and applied to whole views, is a product rule; whether it is enough for a given set of dimensions is not decided. (e) The session data the aggregates are computed from are deleted, destroyed or anonymised once the reason to process them ends (m.7(1)); a controller that must register with VERBİS keeps a retention and destruction policy (Yönetmelik m.5(1)) and destroys at intervals of at most six months (m.11(2)). Anonymised aggregates are not personal data, so the Law's retention rules do not bind them. The retention periods themselves — for aggregates and for raw telemetry — are not decided. Stop-and-ask: consent scope, the retention periods and the DPA's controller-authorisation term are the owner's under CLAUDE.md; this lane gives one recommendation, on consent scope.

Legal basis: KVKK 6698 m.3(1)(a), (b), (ğ); m.4(2)(c)-(ç); m.6(3)(a), (e); m.7(1); m.28(1)(b); Kişisel Verilerin Silinmesi, Yok Edilmesi veya Anonim Hale Getirilmesi Hakkında Yönetmelik m.5(1), m.10(1)-(2), m.11(2); the Kurul's anonymisation guide (Kasım 2017) §3.1 and §3.1.3; KSVY m.16(1)-(2).

## What stays draft

Six parts are not answered by any retrieved text. The legal minimum cell size: no Turkish text sets a number, and the Kurul's guide names the method without a k; a Kurul decision on aggregate health statistics, or counsel's opinion for the panel's actual dimensions, would settle whether the product floor of ten is enough. The retention periods for the aggregates and for raw telemetry: a data-retention decision for the owner, settled in the controller's retention and destruction policy (Yönetmelik m.5-6). The DPA wording that authorises aggregates for the vendor's own improvement: a vendor-contract term for the owner and counsel. The m.28(1)(c) route for record-level research: HB-L26, counsel. The consent-scope decision itself: the owner's, on this lane's one recommendation.

While draft: Nothing reaches the admin panel but aggregates that pass the existing k-anonymity gate at its product floor of ten distinct people, whole views only; no record-level, pseudonymised, per-session or per-patient view is built; in the hospital deployment no aggregate is computed for the vendor's own use until the DPA authorises it; retention periods stay as files/06 §3 states them until the owner decides; and the build half of INT-484 waits for this decision's verdict.
