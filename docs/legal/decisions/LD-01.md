# LD-01 — Adli Tıp: the report-purpose item, and whether the Sağlık Raporları Yönetmeliği governs forensic reports

Scope: synthetic and test use only (owner decision D25, 3 Oct 2026). A licensed Turkish lawyer countersigns before any real patient.

## Question

The allied-and-reports cluster carries Q3, the report purpose (rapor_amaci), to seven allied branches including Adli Tıp. May it be carried to Adli Tıp, where the purpose is forensic? Does the Sağlık Raporları Yönetmeliği (the cluster's source AL-S1) govern forensic reports at all, and does its Madde 8(5) bar on stating a report's purpose reach Adli Tıp?

## Decision

The Sağlık Raporları Yönetmeliği governs forensic reports issued by health service providers: it covers every public and private provider (Madde 2(1)) and its Eighth Part, Adli Hekimlik Hizmetleri (Madde 39 to 52), regulates how adli raporlar are drawn up. It is not the only governing text: for an examination connected with a crime, Madde 40(1) sends the examination and the report to 5271 sayılı Ceza Muhakemesi Kanunu and its implementing rules. The report-purpose item is not carried to Adli Tıp. A forensic examination and report are made on the proper request of a competent official authority (Madde 40(4)), and the report must answer the questions that authority asked (Madde 50(4)), so the purpose of an adli rapor is the requesting authority's, stated in its request, not something the person examined declares. An adli rapor rests wholly on the examining physician's own findings, consultations and test results (Madde 50(1)) and is never drawn up on another person's statement (Madde 48(1)), so no answer from a pre-visit intake, a purpose included, may feed an adli rapor. Madde 52(5) also bars referring a person to a private health service provider for an adli rapor. Whether Madde 8(5) as such reaches an adli rapor does not need deciding for the product: Madde 8(5) is written for health reports in general, the content of an adli rapor is set by Madde 50, and under the rule above no intake answer enters an adli rapor at all. The companion-invitation item held in the adli tıp pack stays held: Madde 48(3) makes it the rule that the physician and the person examined are alone during a forensic examination. What an Adli Tıp pre-visit intake may ask in place of Q3 is not decided here.

Legal basis: Sağlık Raporları Yönetmeliği (RG 19/5/2026, 33258) Madde 2(1), 39(1), 40(1), 40(4), 48(1), 48(3), 50(1), 50(4), 52(5); Madde 8(5) read against Madde 50.

Inference disclosure: That a patient-declared purpose 'adds nothing the physician may use' is this author's reading of Madde 40(4) and 50(4) together; neither says it in those words. Applying Madde 48(3), which governs the forensic examination itself, to a pre-visit companion-invitation item is this author's extension: the item invites a companion before the visit, and the rule it conflicts with binds the examination that follows. Whether a private-hospital tenant should run a forensic pre-visit intake at all is not decided; Madde 52(5) is cited only for referrals made to obtain an adli rapor.
