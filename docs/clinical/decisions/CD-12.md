# CD-12 — Head injury on anticoagulant or antiplatelet treatment: NICE NG232 basis, route and recency

Scope: simulated-patient stress testing only (owner decision D18, 2 Oct 2026). A licensed physician's review is required before any real patient.

## Question

V-BVSC-4 concerns a neurosurgery patient who reports a recent head injury while on an anticoagulant or a non-aspirin antiplatelet. Four questions:

1. Should that red-flag proposal be re-based on NICE NG232? The INT-427 verifier names the referral recommendation as 1.2.4.
2. Which recommendation number and wording apply?
3. Where does the rule route?
4. Within what recency of the injury does it apply?

## Decision

Yes. The proposal is re-based on the primary NICE NG232 text (published 18 May 2023; minor changes up to March 2025), retrieved from nice.org.uk on 2026-10-03. The secondary RCEM reproduction (S8) stays as provenance only.

**Recommendation number and wording.** The verifier's number is right. 1.2.4 is the recommendation for community health services, and its wording matches the reproduction. The neighbouring recommendation 1.2.3 covers remote advice services, the setting nearest to a remote pre-visit intake. Both recommendations:

- list "current anticoagulant or antiplatelet (except aspirin monotherapy) treatment" as a risk factor;
- send the person to a hospital emergency department.

The July 2023 update confirms that either treatment counts.

**Route.** NG232 does not put this risk factor in 1.2.2, the list that sends the person by emergency ambulance. The route is therefore `SAME_DAY_ACIL`, not `EMERGENCY_NOW`. Seizure, focal deficit or reduced consciousness since the injury belong to the 1.2.2 list and to their own rules.

**Drug classes.** These come from 1.5.13:

- vitamin K antagonists;
- DOACs;
- heparin and low molecular weight heparins;
- any antiplatelet regimen except aspirin alone.

Aspirin with a second antiplatelet is not monotherapy.

**Evaluation against today's base slot.** The class cannot yet be applied. `meds_allergies.anticoagulant_use` asks "Kan sulandırıcı bir ilaç kullanıyor musunuz?" with evet, hayır or bilmiyorum. It records no drug class, and CD-02 forbids filling it from a drug name. Until a class-level answer exists:

- any "evet" fires the rule. The aspirin-monotherapy exclusion is not applied, which over-triages and fails closed.
- a "bilmiyorum" is UNCLEAR and resolves to the more acute reading, so it fires too.
- "hayır" does not fire.

Two questions stay draft_needs_source:

- how the class is captured;
- whether patients understand "kan sulandırıcı" to include clopidogrel, prasugrel or ticagrelor. This is left to the HB-C6 base-slot author.

**Recency.** NG232 sets none:

- its definition of a head injury has no time limit;
- its risk factors run "since the injury";
- its only time window, the eight hours in 1.5.13, is a CT-timing rule inside the emergency department, and it extends beyond eight hours ("within the hour if they present more than 8 hours after the injury").

The recency window therefore stays draft_needs_source, with one sourced constraint: the signed window must not be the 1.5.13 eight hours.

**Action constraint (active, no time value).** NG232 refers straight to the emergency department, and 1.5.13 times the scan from the injury. The REMOTE action must therefore:

- send the patient to the acil servis without waiting for a later time of day;
- have them travel accompanied by a competent adult;
- have them go by ambulance when needed, otherwise driven by someone else or by public transport, not driving themselves (1.2.6, 1.2.7);
- not reuse the SARI chest-pain action's "Bugün içinde" wording.

**Placement.** The rule goes in `base.life_threat` (CD-13), not the neurosurgery stub. The EK-4 level, the register and the three location scripts stay draft_needs_source.

## Sources

All NICE-NG232 quotes: *Head injury: assessment and early management*, NICE guideline NG232, published 18 May 2023, with minor changes up to March 2025; retrieved 2026-10-03; snapshot `packages/protocols/sources/NICE-NG232.txt`. Recommendations are from https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng232/chapter/Recommendations; the update note is from https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng232/chapter/Update-information.

- **1.2.4:** "Community health services (GPs, ambulance crews, NHS walk-in or minor injury centres, dental practitioners) and inpatient units without an emergency department should refer people who have sustained a head injury to a hospital emergency department, using the ambulance service if necessary, if there are any of these risk factors (see NICE's guidelines on shared decision making and decision making and mental capacity):"
- **1.2.3:** "Remote advice services (for example, NHS 111) should refer people who have sustained a head injury to a hospital emergency department if there are any of these risk factors"
- **1.2.3 / 1.2.4 list items:**
  - "current anticoagulant or antiplatelet (except aspirin monotherapy) treatment"
  - "a persistent headache since the injury"
- **Update information:** "July 2023: In recommendations 1.2.3 and 1.2.4, we clarified that risk factors can include either current anticoagulant or antiplatelet treatment."
- **1.2.2:** "Remote advice services (for example, NHS 111) should refer people who have sustained a head injury to the emergency ambulance services (that is, 999) for emergency transport to the emergency department if there are any of these risk factors"
- **1.5.13:**
  - "For people who have sustained a head injury and have no other indications for a CT head scan, but are on anticoagulant treatment (including vitamin K antagonists, direct-acting oral anticoagulants (DOACs), heparin and low molecular weight heparins) or antiplatelet treatment (excluding aspirin monotherapy), consider doing a CT head scan:"
  - "within 8 hours of the injury (for example, if it is difficult to do a risk assessment or if the person might not return to the emergency department if they have signs of deterioration) or"
  - "within the hour if they present more than 8 hours after the injury."
- **Definition:** "For the purposes of this guideline, a head injury is defined as any trauma to the head other than superficial injuries to the face."
- **1.2.6:** "Ensure people referred from community health services are accompanied by a competent adult during transport to the emergency department."
- **1.2.7:** "If an ambulance is not needed, provided the person is accompanied, public transport or being driven in a car are appropriate means of transport."

  - Positive: the REMOTE action sends the patient to the acil servis, accompanied.

- `head_injury_window_is_not_eight_hours`
  - Positive: once a window is signed, an injury nine hours ago on a DOAC is still inside it.
  - Near-miss negative: the window value 8 h is refused by the compiler for this rule.

## What stays draft_needs_source and why

- **Recency window.** NICE NG232 states none: its risk factors run "since the injury", and its only time window is an emergency-department CT-timing rule. No Turkish source sets one either.
- **EK-4 level.** EK-4 gives the level of a minor head injury but names no anticoagulant modifier.
- **Base head-injury slot.** Its wording carries the recency, so it waits for the window.
- **Class capture in `anticoagulant_use`.** Today's slot records no drug class. Until it does, the rule fails closed on any evet.
- **Whether "kan sulandırıcı" is understood to include clopidogrel, prasugrel or ticagrelor.** This is left to the HB-C6 base-slot author.
- **Register, Turkish action wording and the REMOTE / ON_SITE / UNKNOWN scripts.** These are authored with the `base.life_threat` rule by the HB-C2 author.

Until these are signed, the rule is not compiled into a served pack.
