# CD-18 — Clinician parts of V-A-2 (postponement window) and V-GC-4 (whole-body sweating)

Scope: simulated-patient stress testing only (owner decision D18, 2 Oct 2026). A licensed physician's review is required before any real patient.

## Question

- **V-A-2.** Anesteziyoloji Q3d records a recent cold or febrile illness before surgery. Does the pre-visit ever rule on a postponement window?
- **V-GC-4.** Göğüs cerrahisi Q11c asks whether excessive sweating is also all over the body. Is that item clinically right, and how is it read?

## Decision

### V-A-2 (anaesthesiology): no, the pre-visit never rules on a postponement window

The pre-visit never rules on postponing or proceeding. Q3d stays a planning fact for the anaesthesiologist and the surgeon. It raises no rule, no flag and no patient-facing advice.

The current Turkish source is TARD's February 2025 paediatric preoperative guideline. It:

- lists a current or recent upper respiratory infection as a risk factor;
- puts the decision to proceed or postpone with the anaesthesiologist and the surgeon, through risk stratification and family-centred decision-making;
- suggests at least two weeks for a child with a severe infection, graded on findings the clinician assesses;
- states that clear evidence on how long to postpone is still lacking.

TARD's February 2025 adult guideline has no statement on postponing after a respiratory infection. The one-month figure in the proposal came from TARD's 2015 preoperative document. TARD Akademi's current list replaces that document with the two February 2025 guidelines, so the figure is not used.

Telling a patient to postpone an operation is treatment language, which PolicyGuard forbids in patient copy.

Q3d's wording stays as it is (it is author-owned), and its source line moves to the 2025 paediatric guideline. The adult window stays draft_needs_source, and nothing reads it.

### V-GC-4 (thoracic surgery): Q11c is confirmed

NICE HTG339:

- defines primary hyperhidrosis of the upper limb as excessive sweating of the palms or axillae;
- limits endoscopic thoracic sympathectomy to severe, debilitating primary hyperhidrosis that has not responded to other treatments.

Sweating all over the body is the fact a patient can report that separates generalised sweating from that focal pattern. That is why it bears on surgical candidacy. It is read as context, never as an eligibility rule.

HTG339 also says that sweating elsewhere on the body is usual after the procedure, a sympathectomy. Q8 records any previous chest surgery and does not identify the procedure. A Q11c "evet" is therefore shown beside the Q8 answer, and it is never read as post-sympathectomy sweating unless the patient names a previous sympathectomy.

The same HTG339 recommendation makes earlier treatment part of candidacy, and the pack does not ask about it. One gated item is added: Q11d, whether antiperspirants, tablets or injections were tried.
