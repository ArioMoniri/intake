# CD-111 — Cardiac RED do-not lines and the unanswered location wait

Machine-readable record: `packages/protocols/decisions/CD-111.yaml`. Status: **active** (one value, the wait, stays draft_needs_source). Scope: simulated-patient stress testing only (owner decision D18, 2 Oct 2026). A licensed physician's review is required before any real patient. The rule's gate, tense, level, route, register and location branches are untouched, and nothing extends base.life_threat.

## Question

Which of the cardiac RED rule's do-not lines are kept, changed or removed, on what current source, and does a blanket "Bir şey yiyip içmeyin." conflict with the care a 112 call handler or the acil servis gives? And what wait, if any source states one, applies before an unanswered location question resolves to REMOTE with 112?

Origin: INT-468 (GitHub #805), from the INT-465 (#796) clinical re-review of 3 Oct 2026: CD-24 kept the generic do-nots and the no-answer wait draft_needs_source, and the served cardiac rule carries the three do-not lines with no source.

## Decision

REMOTE: "Kendi aracınızla yola çıkmayın." changes to the sourced "Hastaneye kendiniz araba kullanarak gitmeyin."; "Bir şey yiyip içmeyin." is removed; "Tek başınıza kalmayın; yanınızdaki kişiye durumu söyleyin." changes to the prohibition "Yanınızda biri varsa sizi yalnız bırakmasına izin vermeyin." (round 2: every do-not line is a bare negative imperative plural). ON_SITE: "Poliklinik sırasında beklemeyin." and "Tek başınıza gitmeyin — yanınızdaki kişiden ya da bir görevliden yardım isteyin." are kept; "Bir şey yiyip içmeyin." is removed. The nil-by-mouth line contradicts current care: chest-pain first aid (ERC 2025, as Resuscitation Council UK publishes it) and the NHS have the person take aspirin by mouth while emergency help is awaited, NICE CG95 has a clinician offer aspirin as soon as possible and records it when given before arrival at hospital, and the 999 call handler gives the advice on what to do. Nothing replaces it: a medicine instruction is the call handler's or the clinician's to give, after an allergy check the device cannot make. The wait: no current source states a number, so it stays draft_needs_source with the invariant-17 literal, and the device's constant stays an unsigned engineering value; the qualitative rule is decided: silence is not reassurance, so an unanswered question takes the branch that does not withhold 112 (REMOTE with 112 shown), the clinic notification being the only channel that reaches a patient who cannot act; a late "evet" switches to ON_SITE; CD-24's ask-first verdict stands.

**Inference disclosure.** The sources fix the constructs: do not drive yourself; the call handler advises what to do; aspirin by mouth while help is awaited, after an allergy check; rest; someone stays with the person; escort to the acil servis; silence may be someone who can no longer communicate. These choices are this author's, made on those sources: narrowing the driving line from "your own vehicle" to driving oneself, which leaves being driven by someone else not ruled out by the device (the call handler, not the device, decides any other transport; a physician weighs this before any real patient); rendering "stay with the person" for the patient as a conditional prohibition, so a patient alone is not given an instruction they cannot keep or sent to look for someone instead of resting; removing the nil-by-mouth line without a replacement; the concern that a blanket nil-by-mouth line in the hospital could make a patient decline aspirin offered by the acil servis, which is this author's reasoning, not a source's; and the late-answer switch. The Turkish wording of the two changed lines is this author's B1 rendering, PolicyGuard-clean.

### The unanswered location question

- **Rule (active).** An unanswered location question resolves to REMOTE with 112 shown, never to silence and never to ON_SITE: silence after an emergency contact is not reassurance, so the device takes the branch that does not withhold 112. A 112 button summons nothing for a patient who cannot act; the clinic notification (notify_clinic: always) is the only channel that reaches them. CD-24's ask-first verdict stands: 112 is not shown during the wait, because showing it first guesses REMOTE.
- **Late answer (active).** An answer that arrives after expiry still decides the branch: 'evet' switches to ON_SITE (112 withdrawn, staff notified, the ON_SITE script shown), as answering the location re-derives it (files/02 §5.3); 'hayır' keeps REMOTE. LD-05 (docs/legal/decisions/LD-05.md, on the INT-471 branch) settles how the late answer is displayed and is cited once it is on main.
- **The wait (draft_needs_source).** Value: the invariant-17 pending literal (see the YAML record). No retrieved current source states how long to wait for an answer before taking the no-answer branch. Searched on 3 Oct 2026: the EENA silent-calls operations document (no timing; it says what silence may mean), NICE CG95 1.2.3, the ERC 2025 first aid guideline as RCUK publishes it, the NHS heart attack page, files/02 §5.3 and files/04 §8.1 rule 7 (they fix the branch, not a wait), and EK-4, whose only time figure is a treatment target, not a question timeout. WCAG timing is the legal lane's. Any number here would be invented.
- **Meanwhile.** While no_answer_wait is draft, the device's constant (UNSIGNED_LOCATION_NO_ANSWER_WAIT_MS on #796) stays an unsigned engineering value, recorded as such (CD-24); on expiry the branch is REMOTE with 112 shown, never silence, and the clinic notification fires in every case.
