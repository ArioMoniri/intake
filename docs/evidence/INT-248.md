# INT-248 — PolicyGuard precision against the real Turkish catalogue

CLAUDE.md invariant 2 (PolicyGuard), invariant 10 (scripted text is verbatim),
invariant 21 (RED uses the bare imperative plural). files/04 §8.

## The report

A localization audit ran `PolicyGuard` over the shipped copy and returned
**23 findings**, asserting that none was a real copy defect. Three claims were
put up for verification:

1. The `sen`-register detector matched `\w+(?:din|sın|sin)\b`, which also
   matches the **polite imperative** `edin` ("Devam edin") and the
   **third-person optative** `ulaşsın`.
2. `\btanı\s+koy` matches the negated disclaimer "Vizite tanı koymaz".
3. All 23 are guard false positives, none a real copy defect.

## Verification — reproduced before anything was changed

Corpus: `apps/mobile/src/lib/i18n/tr.ts` (672 entries) and the spoken strings
of `files/protocols/base.life_threat.yaml` (37 strings), scoped to the classes
the audit used — `diagnosis`, `treatment`, `prognosis`, `register`.

| source | findings |
|---|---|
| `tr.ts` | 21 |

**Finding 1 — CONFIRMED.** `_SEN` was
`\b\w+(?:sın|sin|sun|sün|yorsun|dın|din|dun|dün|ecen|acan)\b|…`.
`edin` matched `\w+din` (14 occurrences in the catalogue) and `ulaşsın`
matched `\w+sın`. 17 of the 21 `tr.ts` findings were register, and 15 of them
were the polite imperative `edin`/`gidin`. The product could not say
`Devam edin`.

**Finding 2 — CONFIRMED.** `\btanı\s+koy` matched inside `koymaz`, so
`app.disclaimer` — *"Vizite tanı koymaz."*, the sentence that exists to
satisfy invariant 2 — was reported as a diagnosis claim.

**Finding 3 — CONFIRMED for the classes flagged.** All 23 are guard false
positives. Two independent reads agreed. The causes:

| cause | strings |
|---|---|
| polite imperative `-In` on a /t/-final stem (`edin`, `gidin`) | 15 |
| third-person optative `-sIn` (`ulaşsın`, `eklensin`) | 2 |
| RED action script `Acil Servis'e gidin` | 2 |
| negated `tanı koymaz` | 1 |
| non-clinical `kullanın` / `bırakın` (app build, date field, app usage) | 3 |

**RED scripts — AFFECTED, and this is the serious half.** Findings 22 and 23
are the `ON_SITE` `action_tr` of `rf.chest_pain_cardiac_features` and
`rf.chest_pain_no_cardiac_history`: *"**Acil Servis'e gidin[ — …]**.
Randevunuzu beklemeyin."* CLAUDE.md invariant 21 and the clinical content
rules make the bare imperative plural mandatory inside RED precisely because
ambiguity is the harm there. The guard was flagging the one register the spec
requires. `Tutun`, `Arayın` and `Bakmayın` themselves were *not* matched —
`-tun`/`-yın` were absent from the old pattern — so the defect reached RED
through `gidin` alone, which is why a syllable-list looked like it was working.

**A 24th finding the audit did not report.** `health.attach.confirm` —
*"Bu özet, vizit notunuzun taslağına eklensin mi?"* — was also counted as
`multi_question`. One question, one mark: `_question_count` split on the comma
and found the *main clause* interrogative behind a fronted subject. Fixed
here, since it is the same defect.

## Patterns changed

### 1. `_SEN` → six morpheme-anchored rules

**Before** — one alternation of final syllables:

```python
_SEN = re.compile(
    r"\b\w+(?:sın|sin|sun|sün|yorsun|dın|din|dun|dün|ecen|acan)\b"
    r"|\bsen\b|\bsana\b|\bseni\b",
    re.IGNORECASE,
)
_SIZ = re.compile(r"\b\w+(?:sınız|siniz|…|ünüz)\b")  # stripped first
```

**After** — `_SEN_RULES`, each demanding positive evidence of the second
person singular. `_SIZ` is deleted: every rule is `\b`-anchored, and the `siz`
forms (*misiniz*, *geleceksiniz*, *gördünüz*) run past the boundary a `sen`
form stops at, so the pre-strip is no longer load-bearing.

| rule | pattern | catches | rejects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_SEN_PRONOUN` | `\b(?:sen\|sana\|seni\|senin\|sende\|senden\|seninle\|seninki\|sensiz)\b` | Sen, Senin, Sende, Senden | — (no homograph exists) |
| `_SEN_CLITIC` | `\b(?:mısın\|misin\|musun\|müsün)\b` | "İyi misin?" | "İyi misiniz?" |
| `_SEN_FINITE` | TAM + person: `yorsun`, `(acak\|ecek)sın`, `(mış\|miş\|muş\|müş)sın`, `(malı\|meli)sin`, `rsın`, `(maz\|mez)sın`, `(rsa\|rse\|mazsa\|mezse)n` | hissediyorsun, hissedeceksin, düşmüşsün, dinlenmelisin, alırsın, yapamazsın, istersen | **ulaşsın, eklensin, gönderilsin, gelsin, olsun** — no TAM morpheme; doksan, seksen |
| `_SEN_PREDICATE` | closed set of address predicates + `-sIn` | hastasın, nasılsın, yorgunsun | ulaşsın, eklensin |
| `_SEN_PAST` | voicing assimilation: `(?:[pçtkfhsş]t\|[voiced]d)[ıiuü]n\b` minus a closed homograph set | baktın, gittin, geldin, uyudun, yaşadın, başladın | **edin, gidin**, altın, bütün, metin, hizmetin, anlatın, kapatın, yönetin, tutun, satın, kadın |
| `_SEN_IMPERATIVE` | verb-stem inventory + clause-final lookahead | "Bana anlat.", "Şuraya bak.", "Oraya bakma.", "Derin bir nefes al." | anlatın, bakın, bakmayın, tutun, arayın, gidin, beklemeyin |

The morphology, stated once: **`-DI` surfaces as `-t` only after a voiceless
obstruent and as `-d` only after a voiced sound.** That single rule rejects
*altın*, *bütün*, *metin*, *hizmetin*, *satın*, and the polite imperatives
*anlatın*, *kapatın*, *yönetin*, *tutun* — a `t` after a vowel is not a
past-tense suffix — without naming any of them.

Two closed classes survive the phonology and are named in the code, each with
its justification:

* `edin gidin tadın güdün didin` + the fused compounds of *et-* — the
  exhaustive set of Turkish verb stems ending in the alveolar stop that voices
  to /d/ before the vowel-initial polite imperative `-In`. Fixed by the
  lexicon; it cannot grow with new copy. Excluded by whole token, because
  *yedin*, *dedin*, *söyledin* and *istedin* also end in `edin` and **are**
  genuine second-person pasts.
* `kadın aydın` — the two everyday nouns homographic with the suffix; Turkish
  has no stems \*ka- or \*ay-. Not hypothetical: `Kadın Hastalıkları ve Doğum`
  is a branch name in the shipped taxonomy, and the old pattern flagged it.

Known residual, left in deliberately and documented in the code: *üstün*
satisfies the s+t phonology. It does not occur in intake copy, and an
over-trigger on scripted text is a report, never silence.

Deliberately **not** in the bare-imperative inventory, with the reason in the
code: `al`, `ara`, `aç`, `iç`, `yaz` — noun/adjective homographs that can
stand clause-finally in ordinary copy. `nefes al` is in, because the host
disambiguates it.

**Negative controls** (`test_a_genuine_sen_address_is_still_caught`, 26 cases;
`test_the_polite_plural_passes_and_its_singular_twin_does_not`, 10 pairs):

| Acil Servis'e gidin. | Acil servise git. |

| Kadın Hastalıkları ve Doğum · Bütün gün sürdü mü? · Bu metin… · Hizmetin kapsamı… · Doksan gün… | hissediyorsun · alırsın · yapamazsın · hissedeceksin · düşmüşsün · dinlenmelisin · istersen · baktın · gittin · uyudun · yaşadın · yorgunsun · hastasın |

Coverage strictly increases: the pronoun genitive/locative/ablative, the
conditional, the voiceless past (`baktın`, `gittin`) and every bare imperative
were **missed** by the old pattern and are caught now.

### 2. `_DIAGNOSIS` — `koy` gains a negative lookahead

**Before** `(?:\bteşhis\s+koy|\btanı\s+koy|…)`
**After** `(?:\b(?:teşhis|tanı)(?:sı|sını)?\s+koy(?!m)|…)`

Turkish attaches both the negative (`-ma/-me`) and the infinitive
(`-mak/-mek`) directly to the stem, and no affirmative finite form does — so
one lookahead excludes `koymaz`, `koymuyor`, `koymak` by a general
morphological fact rather than by listing them. The `(?:sı|sını)?` addition
widens the match to `tanısı koydum`.

**Negative controls** — must pass: `Vizite tanı koymaz.` ·
`Bu uygulama teşhis koymaz.` · `Vizite size tanı koymuyor.` ·
`Burada tanı konmadı.` · `Doktorunuz size bir tanı söyledi mi?`
Must still be caught: `Size teşhis koyabilirim.` · `Bu bir migren atağı.` ·
`Kalp krizi geçiriyorsunuz.` · `Doktorunuz gibi ben de tanı koyarım.` ·
`Bu şikâyete migren tanısı koydum.` (the last was **missed** before).

### 3. `_TREATMENT` — the imperative is gated on a clinical object

**Before** one pattern; `kullanın`/`bırakın`/`alın` fired on any object.
**After** `_TREATMENT_IMPERATIVE and _CLINICAL_OBJECT`, plus an ungated
`_TREATMENT_OFFER` for `tedavi öneriyorum`, which names the treatment itself.
`_CLINICAL_OBJECT` is a medicine, a dose form, or a dosing instruction
(`ilaç`, `hap`, `tablet`, `doz`, `damla`, `antibiyotik`, `ağrı kesici`,
`tedavi`, `aşı`, `insülin`, `günde`, `kez`, `mg`, `ml`, …).

**Negative controls** — must pass: `…boş bırakın.` (a date field) ·
`Tam sürümü kullanın…` (the app build) · `Vizite'yi kullanmayı bırakırsanız…`
(app usage) · `Bölümleri kontrol edin.` · `Düzenli kullandığınız bir ilaç var
mı?` · `Daha önce bu şikâyet için tedavi gördünüz mü?`
Must still be caught: `Günde iki kez ağrı kesici alın.` ·
`Bu ilacı kullanmayı bırakın.` · `Size bir tedavi öneriyorum.` ·
`İlacınızın dozunu artırın.` · `Antibiyotiğinizi hemen kullanın.` ·
`Bu hapı günde bir kez alın.`

### 4. `_question_count` — a fronted subject is not a second question

A *second* question needs a first, so a comma splits questions only where the
fragment **before** it already asked one. Turkish fronts the subject with a
comma constantly.

**Negative control** — must pass: `Bu özet, vizit notunuzun taslağına eklensin
mi?` (1). Must still be caught: `Şu anda hastanede misiniz, yanınızda biri var
mı?` (2), and `Nereniz ağrıyor? Ne zaman başladı?`.

## Real copy defects found — reported, not fixed here

None of the 23 is a defect in the class the guard assigned. Two genuine
defects were found alongside them. Neither is in scope for this issue —
`apps/**` is out of scope, and red-flag rule semantics need a clinician —
so both are reported rather than changed.

1. **`why_tr` on two RED rules makes a hedged statement about the patient's
   condition.** `files/protocols/base.life_threat.yaml` line 141 states the
   rule in the pack's own DSL header: *"`why_tr`: the explanation. Never a
   diagnosis."* But `rf.chest_pain_cardiac_features` (lines 207, 224) says
   *"Anlattığınız belirtiler kalple ilgili olabilir"*, and
   `rf.paediatric_airway_obstruction` (lines 422, 440) says *"Nefes yolu
   daralmış olabilir."* Both attribute the symptoms to an organ system. The
   file's own comment at lines 209–211 concedes the risk class by praising the
   adjacent 112 sentence for being *"a statement about the service, not about
   the patient's condition"*. The paediatric rule is `draft_needs_source` and
   cannot reach a served pack; **the chest-pain one is fully sourced and would
   compile.** PolicyGuard does not detect hedged attribution and was not
   extended to — growing the clinical lexicon is a clinical review
   (CLAUDE.md), so this needs a clinician's decision.
2. **`-in`/`-iniz` is wrong in instructional prose, catalogue-wide.**
   CLAUDE.md and files/00 §2 require `-in` for short in-flow buttons and
   `-iniz` for instructional prose. `apps/mobile/src/lib/i18n/tr.ts` contains
   **no** `-iniz` imperative anywhere; roughly 41 strings use `-in` in
   instructional prose. Eight of them are among the 23 (`auth.phone.invalid`,
   `auth.error.code`, `auth.error.network`, `visits.new.dateOptional`,
   `visits.new.smsFilled`, `wellbeing.intro`, `voice.transport.needsDevBuild`,
   `review.intro`); the other ~33 are at lines 37, 41, 46, 47, 51, 52, 79, 87,
   90, 91, 94, 97, 98, 101, 135, 137, 138, 142, 144, 153, 157, 160, 529, 539,
   540, 583, 612, 662, 682, 695, 760, 782, 806. The eleven flagged strings
   that are **buttons** are correct as they stand. This is a copy-style rule,
   not a PolicyGuard safety class, and fixing only the eight the guard
   happened to hit would leave the catalogue less consistent than it is now.

Also noted, for whoever owns the spec: the inversion clause in CLAUDE.md
("Invert inside RED…") is written as scoped to RED, but
`rf.chest_pain_no_cardiac_history` is `SARI_K1`/`CALM` and its action script
correctly uses the same bare imperative plural. The clause should be scoped to
action scripts and do-not lists generally, not to `level: KIRMIZI_*`.
