## 4. The self-logging read — the decision and why

**The problem, stated exactly.** Reading an access log is itself an access to
clinical metadata. Log it and show it, and every read adds a row the next read
displays: the log grows by being looked at, and a patient who opens the screen
twice sees their own two visits to it. Log nothing, and there is a hole —
an insider (or a `support` grant, or break-glass) could enumerate a patient's
access log leaving no trace, on the one surface whose entire purpose is that
nothing is untraced.

**Decided: log always, project never — for self only.**

1. Every access-log read appends **exactly one** chain entry,
   `action = "api.access_log.read"`, `resource_ref = patient:<id>`,
   `consent_scope_ref = scope:core`. The chain stays complete: the auditor, the
   `auditor` role's evidence export and the INT-037 DSAR export all read the
   chain, not this view, and they see every read.
2. The entry is written **write-ahead** — before the query runs — matching
   files/10 §2's ordering for break-glass ("audit **before** the read"). An
   enumeration that dies halfway has still left its trace.
3. The subject's **own** `api.access_log.read` entries are **suppressed in the
   SQL WHERE clause**, not filtered after the fetch. Post-hoc filtering is a
   real bug, not a style choice: with `LIMIT 50` over a log whose newest 50
   rows are the patient's own reads, a post-hoc filter returns an empty screen.
   `test_suppression_happens_in_the_query_so_a_small_limit_is_not_consumed`
   pins that.
4. A **non-self** access-log read is **not** suppressed. Staff reading a
   patient's access log is precisely the entry files/10 §3 and files/08 §2.5(7)
   want the patient to receive, and it renders as its own `AccessKind.ACCESS_LOG`
   rather than blurring into a generic "view".

Net effect, pinned by `test_reading_the_log_does_not_grow_the_log`: three reads
produce three chain entries and zero additional visible rows. Not a loop, not a
hole.

**Cost, stated rather than hidden:** the patient cannot see their own
access-log reads *in this view*. They are in the chain and in the DSAR export.
If a future decision wants them visible, the change is one predicate, and it
must come with a bounded-growth answer first.

## 5. What a patient sees, and what it costs to say it

| view field | source | note |
|---|---|---|
| `seq`, `occurred_at` | the chain entry | the ISO instant exactly as hashed — never re-serialised |
| `actor_kind` | `self` \| `other` | derived from the requesting principal |
| `actor_role` | `ActorRoleDirectory` | a files/08 §2.1 role id or `null`. **Today it is always `null`** — see below |
| `access_kind` | total rule over the action's dotted segments | `view · share · export · change · erase · consent · refused · access_log · other` |
| `record_kind` | total rule over the ref prefix | `account · visit · note · document · share · consent · request · other` |
| `record_ref` | the chain's `resource_ref` | the subject's own id; nothing else is ever in the set |
| `basis_ref` | the chain's `consent_scope_ref` | `scope:core`, `basis:KVKK-11/13` — already id-shaped, already PHI-free |
| `*_tr_key` | derived | keys, never copy |

**The "who" is honestly empty.** files/10 §3 says the patient-visible entry
carries the actor **role**, not the name. The chain stores an actor *id*, and
there is no account directory in the product to resolve it against: the dev
tier has two synthetic accounts and Keycloak plus the console role grants land
with INT-131/INT-180. So `ActorRoleDirectory` is a seam and the shipped
implementation is `NoRoleDirectory`, which answers "no role is recorded" —
the same posture as `NoAgeRecordSource` for a missing date of birth. A role
that a future directory returns and that is **not** one of the twelve files/08
§2.1 ids is dropped to `null`: an unrecognised "role" is far likelier to be a
person's name, and a patient-facing surface is the last place to discover that
(invariant 6). Pinned by
`test_a_role_outside_the_files_08_role_set_is_dropped_not_rendered`.

**Ownership is a query predicate.** `owned_resource_refs()` builds the closed
ref set from the subject's own rows — `patient:`, `visit:`/`session:` (sessions),
`note:`, `doc:`, `consent:`, `dsar-request:`, `dsar-rectification:`, and
`share:` joined through the note — each SELECT predicated on `patient_id` AND
`tenant_id` inside the tenant session. The route takes **no patient id
parameter at all**, so a caller cannot widen the view; a ref shape not listed
narrows it and can never widen it. An empty ref set returns an empty log rather
than an unfiltered one (`test_an_empty_ref_set_returns_nothing_rather_than_everything`).

**Consent scope (invariant 4).** `GET /v1/me/access-log` declares
`require_scopes("core")` — it is a standing product surface reading clinical
metadata, so it checks a scope like every other such read. It deliberately does
**not** take the `data_subject_right` marker: that cage exists because KVKK
m.11 is exercisable regardless of consent state, and a patient who has withdrawn
`core` and still wants their log has the m.11 route, where the request belongs.
`test_the_route_declares_its_consent_scope` pins both the declaration and
`unguarded_data_routes(app) == []`.

**Fail closed.** With no reader wired the route answers `503
{"error": "access_log_unconfigured"}` rather than an empty list: an empty access
log and an unreadable one look identical to a patient, and only one of them is
true.

## 7. Observability of the verifier — what exists, what a scheduler issue must own

**Delivered here:** `python -m intake_platform.audit_verify`

- `--audit-db-url` (or `$INTAKE_AUDIT_DB_URL`), `--expected-head`, `--metrics-file`
- exit `0` intact · `1` chain broken · `2` unusable invocation. The third is not
  the second: a chain that could not be *read* is an operational failure, and
  reporting it as a tamper finding would page the wrong on-call and teach them
  to discount the real one (`test_an_unreachable_database_is_unusable_not_broken`).
- one JSON object on stdout: `{check, ok, anchored, entry_count, chain_head,
  first_bad_seq, reason}` — ids, counts, seqs and hashes only; never the
  connection string (credentials) and never an entry's content
  (`test_the_report_carries_ids_only`).
- `--metrics-file` writes `sec_audit_chain_ok` in the Prometheus textfile
  format via a **local** `CollectorRegistry`, so importing the module never adds
  a series to a service's `/metrics`.
- `anchored: false` when no external head is supplied. An unanchored chain walk
  cannot catch edit-and-recompute (files/10 §2, corrected 25 Aug 2026), so the
  run says what it did not prove instead of showing a green that overstates it.

**Metrics catalogue row — already present, one field needs changing (file not
owned by this issue).** `observability/metrics_catalog.yaml:986`:

```yaml
  - name: sec_audit_chain_ok
    id: sec.audit_chain_ok
    type: gauge
    labels: []
    owner: security
    unit: "bool (0/1)"
    status: planned      # <-- becomes `live` once a scheduler runs the job
    description: Daily audit hash-chain verification (1 = intact).
```

Nothing uncatalogued is emitted (invariant 13 holds today): the id exists. The
`status: planned → live` flip belongs with the issue that schedules the job,
because `tests/observability/test_metrics_catalog.py::
test_live_entries_are_emitted_and_planned_entries_are_not` reads the *default*
registry and a batch job's local registry is not in it — flipping it now would
make that gate fail.

**Registry probe row — specified, not written (`observability/` and
`services/probes/` are not owned by this issue).** `intake_probes.registry`
validates `probe` against a closed `ProbeId` enum whose members must exist in
`intake_probes.probes`, so the row and the probe function must land together:

```python
# services/probes/src/intake_probes/probes.py
def audit_chain_verified(verify: Callable[[], VerificationResult]) -> Outcome:
    """The files/06 §4 "WORM audit with daily verification" control as a probe.
    `verify` is injected (intake_platform.audit.run_daily_verifier bound to the
    store), so the unit lane never touches a database."""
    result = verify()
    if result.ok:
        return Ok(result.reason)
    return Failed(result.reason)
```

```python
# services/probes/src/intake_probes/registry.py — ProbeId
AUDIT_CHAIN_VERIFIED = "audit_chain_verified"
```

```yaml
# observability/registry.yaml
  - component: audit-chain-verifier
    self_hostable: true
    self_hosted_now: false          # true the moment a scheduler runs it
    host: "in-process batch job: python -m intake_platform.audit_verify"
    license: "n/a - first-party code"
    probe: audit_chain_verified
    budget_ms: 5000
    fallback: "none"
    phi_path: false                 # reads hashes, ids and refs; no PHI
```

**What a `services/workers` scheduler issue must still own — precisely.**
`services/workers` is named in the CLAUDE.md repo map and **no issue in
docs/backlog/issues.yaml creates it**; building a whole worker service under
INT-016 would be scope this issue was not given, and invariant 15 makes it
expensive (a new service without `/healthz`, `/readyz`, `/metrics`, `/version`,
a `docs/slo/<service>.yaml` and a synthetic probe fails `make check` — see
`tests/observability/test_slo_files.py::test_every_service_dir_has_an_slo_file`).
That issue must own:

1. **The service itself**: the Celery app, its worker and its beat schedule, plus
   the invariant-15 surface — the four endpoints from
   `packages/intake_platform/health`, OTel through the shared setup,
   `docs/slo/workers.yaml`, and a synthetic probe.
2. **The daily trigger** — one run per day, invoking `main()` or
   `run_daily_verifier` directly, with the *app* (read-only) engine rather than
   `svc_audit_writer`.
3. **Metric transport**: where `sec_audit_chain_ok` goes for a batch job —
   node-exporter textfile collector (already supported by `--metrics-file`) or a
   Pushgateway — plus the `status: planned → live` flip above and an alert rule
   on `sec_audit_chain_ok == 0`.
4. **The registry row + probe function** exactly as specified above, flipping
   `self_hosted_now: true`.
5. **The external anchor (invariant 40), which is the part that matters most.**
   `--expected-head` is an input here and nothing produces it. The scheduler
   issue (or its own) must publish the chain head **hourly, outside the
   `platform_admin` blast radius** — a second organisation's object store, or a
   KEP/e-imza timestamp from a Turkish TSP reusing the DPA machinery — and feed
   the *external* head back to the verifier. Until then every run is
   `anchored: false` and the flip-and-repair attack in files/08 §0c F5 is
   undetected.
6. **`seq`-gap alerting** (`admin.audit_seq_gaps_total`, catalogued): a clean
   deletion leaves a valid chain, so gaps must alert independently of hash
   failure. `seq` monotonic per actor and per tenant is files/10 §2 (c) and is
   not implemented.
7. **`admin.audit_external_anchor_age_min`** (catalogued, alert at > 90 min).
