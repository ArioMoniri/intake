# Evidence — INT-291 · The patient could not interrupt by speaking

## The report

Owner, verbatim: *"the voice is too robotic and non disruptble at middle and no
emotions"*, after an earlier *"tts is so awkward"* (which INT-237 answered in
part). Three defects in one sentence about one surface — the spoken turn — with
three different owners in the code. The first is a **safety** property and the
rest of this issue is subordinate to it.

sec_row: **`intake-voice`** (docs/security/threat-model.md). That row's assets
already include *"the RED delivery path, patient trust in the voice"* and its
trust boundary is *"patient's room ↔ the session"*. This issue adds the threat
it was missing — **[D] the patient cannot stop or correct the agent** — plus the
controls, the signal (`voice.barge_in_stop_ms`, planned → live) and the tests.

**CLAUDE.md invariant 20.** Every number below is a measurement of two dev
daemons on one host on one day, with the date and the corpus named. None of it
is a property of either model, and none of it appears in `docs/regulatory/`,
`docs/legal/` or patient copy.

## (a) What actually happened when a patient spoke over the agent

**Nothing. There was no microphone.**

`apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/useDialogueSession.ts`, speaking driver, before
this change:

```ts
await AudioModule.setAudioModeAsync({ allowsRecording: false, playsInSilentMode: true });
…
player.play();
```

and the recorder was started only by the *listening* driver, which runs on
`phase === "listening"`. So for the entire duration of every agent clip the
audio session forbade recording and no recorder existed. The two interrupts
that did exist were both manual:

| interrupt | how | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| tap the speaking bubble | `DialogueIntake.tsx` `onPress={session.bargeIn}` | `stopPlayback()` then `dispatch({type:"barge_in"})` |
| type into the composer | `sendTyped` | `stopPlayback()` — the comment even says *"typing over agent speech is a barge-in"* |

files/02 §8.2 budgets *"Barge-in stop ≤ 150 ms"*. There was nothing to measure
it against unless a finger was involved. On the runtime side the gap was the
same shape: `AudioSink` is documented *"Outbound only — the Talker holds no
inbound frames at all"*, and no module took a patient-speech event.

### After

Three things, and the third is the one that matters most:

1. **The microphone opens when the agent starts speaking**, not when it stops
   (`allowsRecording: true`, `shouldRouteThroughEarpiece: false` — stated, not
   defaulted, because `.playAndRecord` is also what puts iOS audio on the
   receiver).
2. **A level watch feeds a pure onset detector** (`bargeIn.ts`) at
   `BARGE_IN_SAMPLE_MS` = 50 ms, reading `recorder.getStatus().metering` — a
   scalar the platform already computes. On `"fired"` it calls **the same
   `bargeIn`** the tap calls, so there is one reducer action and one state
   machine, not two.
3. **The recording that hears the interruption IS the patient's answer.** The
   listening driver no longer re-prepares a recorder that is already running.
   A barge-in whose opening words are lost is a patient made to repeat
   themselves.

### The measured stop

`tests/voice/test_barge_in.py`, on the **shared virtual clock** INT-092/093/110
use (`tests/voice/talker_harness.py::FakeClock`) and INT-093's own `MediaPlane`
double, whose `play` is interruptible on that clock so *"the playback was cut"*
is **observed** rather than asserted. Zero wall time; **no test sleeps**.

| figure | definition | measured | budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **stop** | speech onset detected → outbound audio cancelled | **40.0 ms** (cancel charged 40 ms) | ≤ **150 ms** |
| **stop, at the boundary** | cancel charged exactly the budget | **150.0 ms** — inside, by the spec's own `≤` | ≤ 150 ms |
| **stop, missed** | cancel charged 190 ms | **190.0 ms**, `BargeInRefusal.STOP_BUDGET` recorded | — |

`test_the_stop_is_measured_from_DETECTION_not_from_arrival` fires the onset a
virtual minute into the session, so a path that stamped `now()` on arrival —
silently discounting the uplink hop from the budget it polices — reads wrong
rather than reading the same. `test_a_patient_speaking_mid_sentence_cuts_the_
playback_it_found` starts 400 virtual ms of agent speech, fires at 100 ms, and
asserts the clip landed in `interrupted` and **not** in `completed`.

**One honest limit.** This is the *server-side* stop. What the patient hears is
additionally bounded by the device's own flush of already-buffered audio, which
is on-hardware evidence. Calling this figure patient-perceived would be exactly
the accuracy claim invariant 20 forbids — the same limit INT-093 recorded for
its own stop.

### Three decisions inside that path, each deliberate

**A missed budget is recorded, not raised.** INT-093 raises `RedPathViolation`
after delivering, and is right to: a missed RED budget is a safety event. A
barge-in that stopped 40 ms late is a *quality* event, and raising would tear
the session down — taking the conversation away from the patient who just asked
for it. So the violation travels on `BargeInOutcome` and the caller decides.
`test_a_clean_stop_records_no_violation` is the negative control.

**RED outranks the patient's own voice.** Invariant 21: inside RED the register
inverts to the bare imperative plural *because ambiguity is the harm*. A patient
talking over *Tutun · Arayın · Bakmayın* must not silence the three words that
tell them what to do, so a barge-in arriving while a RED script is in flight is
**refused** — explicitly and on the record, never a silent drop (invariant 17) —
and `test_the_refusal_lifts_when_the_red_script_has_finished` pins that it is a
gate and not a session kill.

**It cannot become a preemption.** Invariant 9 gives the Talker one preemption.
A barge-in is a patient-originated transport event, so
`test_the_path_is_not_a_preemption_and_says_so_in_its_own_source` parses
`barge_in.py`, strips every docstring with `ast`, and asserts `Priority`,
`BackendResult`, `WorkTicket`, `WorkRequest` and `WorkSubmitter` appear nowhere
in the *code*. Prose about the preemption path is documentation; only code is a
claim. `test_the_path_holds_no_audio` does the same for `bytes`/`frames`/`pcm`
(invariant 3) and pins `SpeechOnset` to exactly `{utterance_id, detected_at_ms}`
— there is no field for a transcript, so a caller cannot put patient words on
the signal by accident (invariant 6).

### The metric

`voice.barge_in_stop_ms` moves **planned → live** in
`observability/metrics_catalog.yaml`, emitted from
`intake_voice_runtime.metrics.BARGE_IN_STOP_MS` with files/08 §4 group B's own
label set `{tenant, profile, branch, vendor}` — all four closed and
non-identifying. It stays a **separate series** from `preempt.red_latency_ms`,
which is docs/evidence/INT-093.md's stated reason held as a fact about the code
(`test_the_two_stop_causes_stay_on_two_series`):

> *"that series is the patient's barge-in, and folding a second cause into it
> would make its ≤ 150 ms target unreadable for both."*

`tests/observability/test_metrics_catalog.py` enforces the promotion from the
other side: it is bidirectional, so a family declared in source that is still
catalogued `planned` fails, and so does the reverse.

### The self-echo problem

files/00_STATE.md §2 records the AEC/AGC collision: with the mic open on a
speakerphone the agent hears itself. A fixed threshold would make the agent cut
itself off mid-question — the worst possible answer to "the voice is robotic" —
and there is no API at this tier that separates the agent's echo from the
patient. So the threshold is **measured per clip**:

| constant | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `ARM_DELAY_MS` | 400 | deaf while the opening word plays; the patient's answer to the *previous* turn is often still in the air |
| `CALIBRATION_MS` | 400 | the loudest sample in this window is this room's echo-and-noise floor, for this clip, at this volume |
| `ECHO_MARGIN_DB` | 9 | ~2.8× amplitude; under ~6 dB it is inside the agent's own level variation across a sentence |
| `MIN_TRIGGER_DBFS` | −40 | a silent room calibrates very low; without a floor the trigger lands under a fan |
| `ONSET_HOLD_MS` | 240 | above the agent's own 200 ms inter-sentence gap, so a reflex landing in that gap is not a turn |

**Every failure mode of that ladder is toward *not* interrupting.** A poisoned
calibration (the patient talks during the window) raises the threshold and the
clip stays tap-interruptible — which is exactly where the product was before
this issue. It never fails toward cutting the agent off. The
`adapts to a LOUD room` test is the pin: the same absolute patient level fires
in a quiet room and does not in a room where the echo is already at that level.

The rule is a pure function on purpose. The one simulator this repo has records
silence, so an energy rule wired straight into the hook would be untestable in
exactly the way that matters.

## (b) VoxCPM2 vs Freya — quality, not only latency

**Date: 2 September 2026.** **Host:** this Mac, both lanes over the SSH tunnel,
both `/health`-green. **Corpus:** eight probes — seven are the agent's own
composed turns, dumped from `apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/speech.ts` over
`apps/mobile/src/lib/intake/questions.ts` (so they are the strings the patient
actually hears, and they are numeral-free by construction), plus the RED-register
bare imperative triplet recorded in CLAUDE.md. **Lanes:**

- `openbmb/VoxCPM2` @ `32279eff`, `voxcpm==2.0.3`, **`"device":"cpu"`**, 48 kHz, normalizer disabled
- `freyavoice/Freya-TTS` @ `d124e074`, code @ `146d36c1`, **`"device":"cuda"`**, normalizer disabled

**The latency gap and the quality gap have different causes, and that is the
finding.** VoxCPM2's own `/health` says `cpu`; Freya's says `cuda`. So the
latency column below is a *placement* artefact of this host, and the spectral
column is not.

to fix this.

### Verdict for the owner's question

**The quality difference is real and it is the cause of "robotic"; the latency
difference is a GPU-placement artefact of this host and not a property of the
model.** VoxCPM2 can produce the 4–8 kHz region Freya structurally cannot, and
round-trips at roughly half Freya's word error on this corpus. On this host it
takes 6.5–46.9 s to synthesise on CPU and cannot meet the 1684 ms budget
files/03 §4.4 states, so INT-249's latency-aware selector is correct and keeps
choosing Freya. **The action is not to change the ladder. It is to give VoxCPM2
the GPU** — and that is blocked on the licence verification invariant 19
requires from the raw LICENSE blob with a recorded `licence_verified_at`
(INT-237 already marked this `awaiting-human` and noted it is not yet carried as
a HUMAN_BLOCKERS row).

## (c) Is numeral expansion a real cause of stilted delivery?

**Yes, in one specific place — and no numeral reaches the surface the owner was
listening to.**

The spoken corpus of the dev-tier dialogue is numeral-free by construction:
`assertSpeakableTr` throws on any Unicode `Nd` before a request leaves the app,
and every option is authored in words (*"Bir haftadan uzun süredir"*). So
expansion is **not** what the owner heard.

On the compiled-protocol path it is, and the example is exact.
`packages/protocols/src/protocols/compile/tr_orthography.py` expands a decimal
fraction **digit by digit**:

```
'Doz 2,25 mg mı?'      ->  'Doz iki virgül iki beş miligram mı?'
'Kilonuz 72,4 kg mı?'  ->  'Kilonuz yetmiş iki virgül dört kilogram mı?'
```

A Turkish speaker says *"iki virgül **yirmi beş**"*. *"iki virgül iki beş"* is
stilted, and it is **deliberate** — the module's own comment says a grouped
reading *"is ambiguous between 2,25 and 2,20-then-5, and an ambiguous reading
cannot be inverted, so the gate could not check it"*. That is a dose-safety
argument and it outranks naturalness. Single-digit fractions (`38,5` →
*"otuz sekiz virgül beş"*) are natural already, so the stiltedness is confined
to two-or-more-digit fractions. **Not changed here**: relaxing it needs the
round-trip gate redesigned, and the fix is certainly *not* re-enabling a
normaliser — Freya reads `2.5 mg` as *"yirmi beş"*, a silent ten-fold dose error.

**One hole found in passing, filed rather than fixed** (it is a compiler issue,
not this issue): `°C` and `mmHg` are absent from `TR_UNITS`, and
`_looks_like_a_unit` only matches slashed forms, so both pass through
**unexpanded** to a TTS whose normaliser is disabled:

```
'Ateşiniz 38,5 °C mi?'  ->  'Ateşiniz otuz sekiz virgül beş °C mi?'
```

files/03 §4.9.6 says *every* unit is expanded at compile time. A separate issue
is owed.

## (d) What changed about prosody, and how it stays the far side of the line

**The line first.** Invariant 2 forbids prognosis language and reassurance *is*
prognosis; PolicyGuard's `_PROGNOSIS` already refuses *geçer · iyileşirsiniz ·
düzelir · ciddi (bir şey) değil · endişelenmeyin · merak etmeyin · prognoz*.
This issue does not go near that guard, and the reason is structural rather
than careful: **the delivery stage cannot originate a word at all.**
`shapeForDelivery — adds no word the input did not carry` asserts, over every
string in the Turkish catalog, that every token of every spoken chunk is a token
of the input (or the same token plus a terminal mark). Warmth here is delivery,
not vocabulary — which is also CLAUDE.md's decided form: warmth comes from verb
mood, not from new sentences. RED delivery is *not* softened; see the floor
below, which makes it more intelligible, not gentler.

Three changes, each measured or blueprint-grounded:

**1 · A measured chunk floor — `MIN_CHUNK_CHARS = 13`.** INT-237 split at
**every** sentence boundary. Measured on the Freya lane the same day, single
fragments synthesised alone and round-tripped:

| chars | fragment | WER | what came back |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | `Tutun.` | 1.000 | *"Tutun, **atun**."* |
| 7 | `Arayın.` | 1.000 | *"Arayın, **arayın**."* |
| 9 | `Bakmayın.` | 1.000 | *"Bakmayın, **ha!**"* |
| 10 | `Onaylayın.` | 1.000 | *"Onaylayın, **hı!**"* |
| 13 | `Bugün başladı.` | 0.000 | clean |
| 20 | `Bugün ne zaman başladı?` | 0.000 | clean |
| 21 | `Kontrol edip onaylayın.` | 0.000 | clean |
| 23 | `Bir haftadan uzun süredir.` | 0.000 | clean |
| 29 | `Söyledikleriniz ekrana yazıldı.` | 0.000 | clean |
| 32 | `Başladığından bu yana nasıl gidiyor?` | 0.000 | clean |

**Every request under 13 non-space characters came back with a token nobody
authored, 4 of 4. The shortest that round-tripped clean was 13.** Confirmed
against a control: `Tutun. Arayın. Bakmayın.` sent as **one** string
round-trips at WER 0.000 (3/3 repeats), and sent as three chunks joined at
0 ms, 280 ms and 600 ms gaps returns the same corrupted string at all three —
so it is the **chunking**, not the gap.

That is not a prosody defect. A model that invents a word breaks invariant 10 at
the acoustic layer: the patient does not hear the string the protocol authored.
And the strings it bites are the shortest and most safety-critical in the
product. So `shapeForDelivery` merges any chunk below the floor into its
neighbour and keeps INT-237's split wherever both sides clear it. Invariant 10
survives the merge by construction — chunks are rejoined with the single space
that already separated them in the collapsed input, so each `text` is still an
exact substring, in order, covering the input completely; the property is pinned
over the whole catalog. Merging can only make a chunk longer.

This **reverses part of INT-237** and says so: INT-237 measured *rate* (the RED
triplet 15.38 → 6.87 c/s) and read that as an improvement, but it never
round-tripped the audio, so it could not see that the three single-word
syntheses are unintelligible. **Intelligibility outranks rate for a safety
script.**

**2 · A turn contour instead of one flat pause.** INT-237 used a single 280 ms
gap at every sentence boundary and trimmed every edge to a flat 15 ms, so the
agent stopped *identically* at a sentence boundary and at the end of its own
turn. A person does neither, and that is the most machine-like property of the
delivery. Both new numbers come from files/03 §4.2's `min_silence_duration ≥
250 ms` end-of-turn floor, in opposite directions:

- `SENTENCE_GAP_MS` **280 → 200**, *below* the floor. A pause inside the agent's
  turn that exceeds 250 ms is, by the runtime's own definition, indistinguishable
  from the agent having finished — and with barge-in now armed that is not
  cosmetic: it invites the patient into the middle of a two-sentence question.
  200 ms is still well above the daemon's fixed 120 ms clause gap, which is what
  INT-237 needed the number for.
- `TURN_YIELD_TAIL_MS` **= 300**, *above* the floor, on the last chunk. Here
  "the turn ended" is exactly what the agent means. It costs no interaction
  latency, because the microphone is open through the whole clip — the patient
  can answer into it.

`bargeIn.test.ts` pins the relation rather than the numbers in isolation:
`ONSET_HOLD_MS` (240) must exceed the inter-sentence gap, so a later change to
either fails there.

**3 · A head-clipped clip is padded, not shaved.** `trimAndFade` trimmed to a
15 ms head and faded 8 ms *into* it. On a lane returning zero leading silence —
VoxCPM2, on 6 of 14 probes — that fade landed on the first phoneme. It now pads
to the kept head when the lead is short, so the fade always lands on silence the
function inserted.
