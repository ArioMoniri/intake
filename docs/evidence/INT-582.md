> count) may be stored, and these patient actions step up to the passkey: issuing a Doctor

>

>

> single-use passkey assertion bound to a server challenge and recorded on the hash chain;

"Never silently let through" and the orchestrator's "a patient without one is NOT locked
out" are the same rule here: a patient without a passkey keeps today's OTP-session tier
**explicitly** — every such pass is audited (`step_up.otp_tier`), counted
(`gate_otp_tier`) and answered with `X-Intake-Step-Up: otp_session` and
`X-Intake-Passkey-Enrolment: offered`, and the app shows the enrolment offer.

## Design notes

**Session binding.** The OTP tier's device session is the DPoP key: `session_ref =
dpop:<jkt>`. A grant minted on one phone is refused on another session of the same patient
(`test_a_grant_from_another_device_session_is_refused`), and the near miss passes (that
session steps up for itself). The dev-bearer tier binds to `bearer:<account>` and cannot
enrol (`passkey_otp_session_required`).

**Counter.** WebAuthn §6.1.1, strictly: 0 against 0 passes (a synced Apple passkey reports
0 forever); otherwise the counter must move forward or the step-up is refused as a clone.
The store's compare-and-set means of two step-ups verified against one counter value,
exactly one moves it (`test_a_counter_race_lost_to_another_step_up_is_refused`).

**Unconfigured.** No `INTAKE_PASSKEY_RP_ID`/`INTAKE_PASSKEY_ORIGINS` (or no OTP tier): nobody
enrols (503); a patient without a passkey keeps the OTP tier with enrolment `unavailable`; a
patient **with** a passkey is refused 503 with the manual path, never downgraded.

**Manual path (KVKK m.11(1)(g)).** Every refusal made on the step-up signal alone carries
`manual_path: "kvkk_m13_application"`: an application to the controller under KVKK m.13,
reviewed by a person. The app renders `passkey.stepUp.refused`. The controller's channel
itself is the legal lane's text, not this issue's.

## Recovery (lost phone) — the design

1. The patient signs in on the new phone by OTP (the tier that exists) and taps
   *Telefonumu kaybettim* → `POST /v1/me/passkeys/recovery` (OTP-session tier only). Every
   passkey with no recovery running gets `recovery_requested_at = now`; asking again does
   not restart or shorten a running one.
2. Through the cool-off the passkey keeps guarding the four actions — the new phone is
   refused with the manual path like any other OTP-only session.
3. The holder of the passkey (the real patient, if the SIM was swapped) cancels with a
   passkey step-up (`recovery_cancel`) → `DELETE /v1/me/passkeys/recovery`. The attacker
   cannot cancel or finish it early (`test_the_passkey_holder_cancels_a_recovery_they_did_not_start`).
4. When the cool-off ends, the next gate or list call deletes the passkey (audited
   `passkey.recovery.complete`), and the patient is on the OTP tier with enrolment offered.

`RECOVERY_COOLOFF_S` is **72 h — an engineering default, not a clinical value, for the owner
to confirm or change** (one constant, `intake_api.passkey.service`). The reasoning: a
SIM-swap attacker must hold the number unnoticed for the whole window, while the real
patient notices a dead SIM within hours and D35's push notice reaches their signed-in
devices (INT-583 builds that channel; until it lands the cool-off itself is the control).

## Inventory annotation — the legal lane's decision

D36 made this a new category and left the annotation to the legal lane. Engineering's first
proposal (KVKK m.5/2-ç, the m.12 security duty) was **replaced by the legal lane's decision in
its review (B1 and B4, 30 Oct 2026)**, which is what the columns now carry and
`tests/data/test_inventory.py` pins, with the near miss that "m.5/2-ç" is absent:

| field | value |
|---|---|
| category | `authentication_credential` (`CAT_AUTHENTICATOR`, in `SUBJECT_DATA_CATEGORIES`) |
| purpose | per column, e.g. "the passkey's P-256 public key every step-up assertion is verified against" |
| basis | `BASIS_ACCOUNT_SECURITY`: KVKK m.5/2-c — necessary for performing the service contract: the optional account-security step-up the patient enrolled in (D36, against SIM swap) |
| retention | `RET_PASSKEY`: until the patient removes it; after a recovery's cool-off at the first gate or list call and at the latest at the next periodic destruction (≤ 6 months); with the record on DSAR erasure |
| recipients | `REC_PASSKEY`: no recipient; processed by the controller only; stored by the hosting processor with the database |

**The m.6 condition** (in the constant's comment and docs/legal/COUNSEL_BRIEF.md): the face or
fingerprint check runs on the patient's phone and no biometric data reaches the controller —
the stored public key is not biometric data — so m.6's special-category condition is not
triggered; were biometric data ever to reach the controller, m.6 would apply and this basis
would not hold. Second condition: if counsel rules the sign-in data is KVKK m.6 health data (INT-535, GitHub #902; docs/legal/drafts/sms-vendor-transfer-memo.draft.md §4), this basis is re-decided with it. The workable ground would then be m.6(3)(a) açık rıza: a separate enrolment text in docs/legal, hash-bound, with removal at least as easy as enrolment. The audit rows keep `basis:KVKK-12` (the chain is the m.12 security record),
which is a different question from the ground for holding the key. Data minimisation: no
AAGUID (device model) is kept, the user handle is 32 random bytes, and the WebAuthn account
label the phone shows is the brand, never the phone number or a name. Counsel's
countersignature is still owed before real patients.
