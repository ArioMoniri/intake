# INT-363 — The kiosk idle wipe warns before it clears and lets the patient extend

**Epic:** E08-mobile · **Phase:** P1 · **Sec-Row:** mobile · **GitHub:** #589 ·
**Legal:** 5378, EN 301 549 · **Depends on:** INT-226 (merged)
**Criterion:** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable (A, Ministry BK13), the "Extend" option

## Acceptance (verbatim)
- "before the kiosk idle timeout clears the session, the patient gets an announced warning with at least 20 seconds to extend with one action"
- "the timing test scans src/lib as well as .tsx screens, so a timer outside a screen is covered"

## Problem
The INT-226 report found two gaps under SC 2.2.1.

1. Kiosk mode clears an armed patient session after 60 s without a touch
   (`KIOSK_IDLE_WIPE_MS`, files/10 §4). This is a time limit the patient
   meets, and it had no warning and no way to extend.
2. The BK13 test read only `.tsx` screens and components. The idle timer
   lived in `lib/kiosk/nativeKiosk.ts`, and its own comment says it was put
   there because the test would not look.

## Change

### The machine (pure, `apps/mobile/src/lib/kiosk/kioskState.ts`)
- **`KIOSK_IDLE_WARNING_MS = 20_000`**, a named constant. W = 20 s is the
  criterion's own floor ("at least 20 seconds"). With T = 60 s left as it was,
  the warning comes after 40 s without a touch. That leaves 40 s of reading
  before any interruption, where 30 s would cut in on a patient reading a long
  question at large text.
- **`kioskIdleReducer`** runs `active → warning → wiped`:
  - `activity` (any touch) and `extend` (the one action) return to `active`
    and restart the full timeout, with no limit on how often;
  - a `tick` that is due moves `active` to `warning` and `warning` to
    `wiped`, and an early tick changes nothing;
  - no path from `active` to `wiped` skips the warning.
- **`kioskIdleDue`** counts the grace from the moment the warning was
  SHOWN, not from the last touch. A late timer can delay the wipe but can
  never cut the patient's 20 s short. With no action and an on-time timer,
  the wipe still falls at exactly 60 s.
- **`startKioskIdleClock(onPhase, timers)`** drives the machine on injected
  timers (`now` / `schedule` / `cancel`). It keeps exactly one timer armed
  for the next due moment and reports each phase change once. It is
  node-tested on a hand-cranked fake clock.

### The adapter (`lib/kiosk/nativeKiosk.ts`)
`watchKiosk` no longer arms its own `setTimeout(onIdle, 60_000)`. It passes
`Date.now` and the platform timer to `startKioskIdleClock` and reports
`onIdlePhase`. It no longer imports `KIOSK_IDLE_WIPE_MS`. `extend()` joins
`touch()` and `stop()`. Backgrounding still wipes and flags a tamper with no
warning, because leaving the app is not a time limit.

### The gate (`components/KioskGate.tsx`)
When `onIdlePhase("warning")` fires:
- A panel appears above the staff bar. It holds
  `<AnnouncedText tone="warning" role="title" message={t("kiosk.idle.warning")} />`
  and one `PrimaryButton`, "Devam edin", whose `onPress` is
  `watchRef.current?.extend()`. Any touch on the armed root also counts as
  activity.
- Screen-reader focus moves onto that button with
  `AccessibilityInfo.sendAccessibilityEvent(extendRef.current, "focus")`, so
  answering takes one double-tap.
- While the warning stands, the session behind it is hidden from the reader
  (`accessibilityElementsHidden` / `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"`).
  Its children stay mounted, so nothing in the intake is lost.

`onIdlePhase("wiped")` calls the same `wipeSession()` as before, and that
function is unchanged. It clears the in-memory notes and extra visits and
crypto-shreds the offline drafts (kiosk.spec's
`wipeSession[\s\S]{0,400}shredDrafts` pin still holds). The effect now also
refuses to start a watch when it was torn down while the adapter was loading.
Before this, a clock started after cleanup would never have been stopped.

### The announcer (`lib/a11y/announce.ts`, `components/Announced.tsx`)
INT-362's announcer had two tones. `error` is assertive and is spoken as
"Hata: …". `status` is polite. A timeout warning is urgent, so it must be
assertive, but it is not an error: the patient did nothing wrong, and saying
"Hata" would misname it. INT-363 therefore adds a third tone, `warning`. It is
assertive: on iOS it posts `{queue: false, priority: "high"}` and on Android
sets the assertive live region. Like a status, it is spoken as shown.
`politenessOf` and `spokenLine` changed by one comparison each. The a11y
tone-literal check accepts the three tones.

### `components/Themed.tsx`
`PrimaryButton` and `SecondaryButton` take an optional `ref` (the React 19
ref-as-prop), passed to their `Pressable`, so that a screen can move focus to
one of them. Both take it so that the shared `ButtonProps` never names a prop
one of them drops.

## The timing scan (the second acceptance line)
`e2e/a11y.spec.ts` BK13 now walks every script under `apps/mobile/src`,
`.ts` included and tests excluded (`APP_SCRIPTS`). It finds each call that arms
a timer, `setTimeout(` or `setInterval(`, with comments stripped; a type such
as `ReturnType<typeof setInterval>` does not count. Every such file must be in
one of two places:
- **`NOT_A_TIME_LIMIT`**, with its reason. There are nine: `otp.tsx` (the
  resend cooldown), request deadlines (`expoApiBase`, `dev/hosts`,
  `georgeClip`, `tts`, `ttsLane`, `extract`), the extraction retry delay, the
  reconnect backoff (`livekit/session`) and the barge-in meter
  (`useDialogueSession`). These match MC-08's reading of each hit.
- **`WARNED_TIME_LIMITS`**, which maps a file to its warning machine. There is
  one entry: `nativeKiosk.ts` → `startKioskIdleClock`. Every timer call in
  such a file must sit inside that machine's argument list. A timer armed
  beside it is caught.

A timer anywhere else is reported as an "unclassified timer". A time limit's
timer outside its machine is reported as "time limit without a warning".
Further tests:
- The walk reaches `src/lib` and finds more than five timers there.
- The register holds no stale entry.
- The old rule stands: no screen or component owns a timer except the
  resend cooldown.
- The constants are pinned. W ≥ 20 000 ms, written out rather than imported,
  so the constant cannot vouch for itself. T is 60 000 ms, and the machine's
  grace after a warning is at least 20 s.

## Tests (TDD)
- `src/lib/kiosk/kioskState.test.ts`: 13 new tests.
  - **The machine on a fake clock (9).** W ≥ 20 s and T = 60 s. The warning
    comes at T − W and not a millisecond before. With no action, the phases are
    exactly `warning@T−W`, then `wiped@T`. A late warning still gets its full
    W. One `extend` restarts the full timeout. Twelve consecutive
    last-millisecond extends never wipe (SC 2.2.1 asks for at least ten). A
    touch during the warning counts. Ordinary use never warns. After the wipe
    nothing is due until the next touch.
  - **`startKioskIdleClock` on a hand-cranked `schedule`/`cancel`/`now` (4).**
    It reports the warning and the wipe once each and leaves nothing armed.
    Extend re-arms the full timeout. A touch restarts silently, never with two
    timers stacked. A stopped clock never reports.
- `src/lib/a11y/announce.test.ts`: 4 new tests. A warning is assertive. It is
  spoken as shown, without "Hata". It posts `{queue: false, priority: "high"}`
  on iOS and nothing on Android.
- `e2e/a11y.spec.ts`: the BK13 section is rewritten, 6 tests replacing 1 (net
  +5). The tone-literal check accepts `warning`.
  - **Near-miss control:**
    - a bare `setTimeout(wipe, 60_000)` planted in `src/lib/fresh/idle.ts` is
      caught;
    - the pre-INT-363 adapter body under its real name is caught;
    - the machine-handed form passes;
    - a second timer beside the machine is caught;
    - a commented timer is ignored.
- `e2e/kiosk.spec.ts`: 5 new source-slice tests.
  - The adapter hands its timer to the machine and no longer names
    `KIOSK_IDLE_WIPE_MS`.
  - The warning is `<AnnouncedText tone="warning">`.
  - The one action is `extend()`, the button carries `ref={extendRef}`, and
    focus is sent to it.
  - The session is hidden from the reader during the warning.
  - `wiped` calls `wipeSession()`, and background still wipes and flags a
    tamper.
