# INT-464: native welcome and sign-in in the stage language

GitHub #793. This is section 2 of the native pass. Decision: ADR-039 **S7**, under the same owner approval as INT-466 (3 Oct 2026, in chat: "yes to all"), recorded on the D23 row. Glass placement follows `docs/design/liquid-glass-research.md`.

## The ask, and how it changed

The first draft of #793 asked for a calm iOS setup layout with "no gradient, blob, emoji or decorative shape". Then the owner approved the chat redesign with "yes to all", and that approval included this instruction: "section 2 (onboarding, #793) in the same visual language".

ADR-039 S7 records what that means on these three screens: the still stage backdrop, the stage orb drawn still as the mark, and glass on the actions only. The backlog row's acceptance is rewritten to S7, and so is the GitHub issue body. The rest of the first draft stands:

- the setup layout;
- the tray;
- the grouped inset list;
- the solid bar with only the back chevron;
- static large titles;
- filled fields;
- no copy changes;
- the consent screen untouched;
- no motion;
- AX5.

## What changed

- **ADR-039 S7, test first.** It bounds welcome, phone entry and OTP entry:
  - nothing moves, because the OTP step is a gate (ADR-033 §4);
  - the mark is `stillOrb()`;
  - the actions are stage buttons with `interactive={false}`;
  - the fields are content, styled by `stageFieldChrome`;
  - the bar is solid, in the wash's first stop;
  - the consent screen is untouched and the copy does not change.

  `tests/docs/test_adr_039.py` pins S7, its decision line and its enforcement row.
- **Pure parts (`lib/stage/`).**
  - `field.ts`: INT-433's filled rule in the stage palette.
  - `nav.ts`: `withSolidStageBar`, which keeps the bar solid, sets it in the wash's first stop, shows only the chevron and keeps the title for the back menu.
  - `orb.ts`: `stillOrb()`.
  - `palette.ts`: a `field` fill. It is light `#FFFFFF`; dark is `#2C4244`, because the dark answer card sits inside the wash's luminance range (see below).
- **Parts.**
  - `useStagePalette()` resolves the palette for scheme, Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency.
  - `StageButton` takes `interactive`. It defaults to true, so the dialogue keeps S4. When false, the press is the instant opacity step.
  - `ListGroup` takes a `surface`.
- **Welcome.** The layout, top to bottom:
  - the still stage backdrop;
  - the still orb mark (112 pt);
  - the static large title, a heading;
  - the tagline;
  - the body;
  - in development builds only, the accounts as a grouped inset list on the stage card, with the header outside and the note as the group's footer;
  - a bottom tray with the one prominent action (inert glass) and the disclaimer.

  The column scrolls (INT-284). The tray rests on the bottom while the column fits.
- **Phone and OTP entry.**
  - The solid bar shows only the back chevron.
  - The step title is a static large-title heading in the content.
  - The fields are filled stage fields with no card. The phone field is a capsule; the six registers keep 12 pt continuous corners.
  - The actions are inert glass.
  - Error lines, the resend cooldown, autofill, `secureTextEntry` and every string are unchanged.

## Found on the simulator, and fixed

- **The orb's glow was cut by its host.** A straight line showed in the dark wash where the glow's blur reached the SwiftUI host's edge.

  - Fix: the orb's host ignores the safe area, as the backdrop's host already did.

## Liquid Glass and capture protection: new evidence

Welcome, phone and OTP do not use INT-131's capture protection, and on them the system glass **draws**: the tinted action shows the glass rim, and the regular actions show the glass material. The same stage button in the capture-protected dialogue shows a flat fill with no rim (`glass-rim-welcome-vs-dialogue.png`).

INT-466's experiments had already changed interactivity, the container, draw timing, remounting and the simulator boot without any effect. Capture protection is the one remaining difference between the two screens.

This supports the INT-131 explanation without switching the protection off. The protection is unchanged, and the decision is still the owner's and security's (#797).
