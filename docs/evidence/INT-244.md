# Evidence — INT-244 · Name the physician of record in the clinical blocker ledger

## Acceptance (verbatim from docs/backlog/issues.yaml)

The clinical sign-off section addressed an anonymous role, so eleven open rows
had no addressee: the owner names the physician of record and every row whose
who column read physician of record or clinician is addressed to him, while the
same edit records that he is a signing clinician rather than a repository user
(the signature is the recorded sign-off fields, attributable in the repository,
not a code review, and the signer needs no account), that most of the rows are
authorship rather than signature so naming the signer produces no content, that
the paediatric row still needs a paediatrician unless he holds that specialty,
and that the sign-off block stays empty until there is content to sign and the
remaining fields are supplied.

## What this does and does not unlock

Recorded in the ledger itself, because the distinction is the point of the
section:

- **A name fills one of six fields.** HB-C1 needs `reviewer_name`,
  `reviewer_licence_no`, `reviewer_specialty`, `signed_commit`, `signed_on`
  and `paediatric_reviewer_name`. The other five are unsupplied and no agent
  may invent them.
- **Most rows are authorship, not signature.** HB-C2 is ~30 adult rules to
  write, HB-C3 a paediatric set that does not exist at all, HB-C4 a pregnancy
  set that does not exist at all, HB-C7 a real number for every
  `TODO: threshold requires clinical sign-off`.
- **HB-C3 still needs a paediatrician** unless Dr Onganer holds that specialty.
- `reviewer_name` is held in the ledger rather than written into
  `clinical_signoff`, because there is not yet content to sign.

## Honest note

Naming an addressee does not advance any clinical row toward closure. It
removes one excuse — "who was supposed to do this?" — and nothing else.
