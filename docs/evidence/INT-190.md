## 1. The design question: where may a number come from?

A cost is a quantity times a price. INT-101 metered the quantities and stopped at the
multiplication because no price exists (invariant 19 leaves no vendor list price for the
runtime; HB-I4/HB-I5 leave no GPU-hour rate). INT-190 does not change that. It gives the price
one home and holds everything else to the catalogue:

| input | where it comes from | what an absence does |
|---|---|---|
| unit price per component | `infra/costs/unit-prices.yaml`, `price.usd_per_unit` + `source` + `as_of` | "price not configured"; the row has no figure and every total it is part of is withheld |
| budget limit per vendor and period | the same file, `limit.usd` + `source` + `as_of` | "budget not configured"; `CostBudgetNotConfigured` fires |
| usage quantity | a **catalogued** series (inv 13), read through a fixed PromQL table | a named state: not metered / not emitted / no samples / source unavailable / source error |
| sessions in the window | `voice_sessions_metered_total` (new, live), from the same meter as the usage | no per-session figure |

The schema refuses a value without a `source` and an `as_of` date, and it also refuses a
`source` without a value, because a half-filled row is a claim nobody made. Every component is
bound to the catalogue at load time. The series must exist, the aggregation must read it as the
type it is, the priced unit must be the unit the series counts, and a vendor-labelled series
must be read for the component's own vendor. A profile-labelled series is spent **directly** by
the profiles the row declares. A series with no profile label is **allocated** evenly across
every session metered in the window, and it may not claim a profile.

**Per-session cost** is usage in a window (1 d or 30 d) divided by the sessions metered in the
same window, times the price. It is a mean, so the surface never needs a session identifier
(inv 6). The divisor is `voice_sessions_metered_total{tenant,profile,branch}`, incremented once
per session by the INT-101 `SessionMeter` when the composition root builds a session around it.
It comes from the same object that emits the usage series, so the numerator and the denominator
describe the same sessions.

### Nothing unknown becomes a zero

- A per-session row has a figure only when the price is configured, the usage was observed for
  that profile, and the divisor is a positive count. Otherwise it carries `None` and its reasons
  in order. An absent profile in a successful answer is "no samples", not 0.
- A per-vendor or per-profile total is either complete or withheld with the list of what is
  missing. A partial sum is never published as the cost.
- A budget's spend is `complete`, a `lower_bound` or `unknown`. A lower bound over the limit is
  `exceeded`, which is sound. A lower bound under it is `not_evaluable`, because it cannot prove a
  budget kept. Only a complete spend can be `within`.
- An alert threshold is `(Σ (usage or vector(0)) × price) > limit` over the priced, metered
  components. Without `or vector(0)`, one silent term would make the whole sum vanish and the
  alert could never fire. With it, the sum is a floor, and a floor above the limit is a real
  overrun. `CostBudgetIncomplete` names what the floor cannot see. For example, the test-only
  priced config generates:

  ```
  CostBudgetExceeded   ((sum(increase(voice_stt_audio_s_sum{vendor="self-hosted"}[1d])) or vector(0)) * 3.0
                        + (sum(increase(voice_tts_chars_sum{vendor="self-hosted"}[1d])) or vector(0)) * 2.0
                        + (sum(increase(voice_talker_tokens_sum{vendor="self-hosted"}[1d])) or vector(0)) * 1.0) > 50000.0
  CostBudgetIncomplete vector(1)   asr: usage series voice_stt_audio_s not emitted yet; llm_talker: …; llm_extraction: usage not metered
  ```

  With the committed file, all eight rules are `CostBudgetNotConfigured` (`vector(1)`,
  `severity: ticket`). They fire until the owner sets limits, because a missing alert would read
  as "within budget".

### The month window and retention

The month budget is a rolling 30 d window. The dev obs Prometheus kept **7 d**, so a month
budget would have compared about a quarter of a month against a monthly limit without saying
so. files/06 §3 already fixes telemetry at **30 d hot**, so the retention flag now reads `30d`,
and `test_retention_covers_the_longest_budget_window` holds the two together. This follows the
retention the legal matrix already states. It is not a new retention decision.

## 5. Where every number ended up

Nothing in this change is a price, a budget or a measurement of the product.

- `infra/costs/unit-prices.yaml` has every `usd_per_unit` and every `limit.usd` set to `null`.
- The only rates and limits in the tree are in `tests/fixtures/cost/unit-prices.priced.yaml`.
  They are whole US dollars per character, token or audio second, chosen to be absurd, and each
  row's `source` reads "synthetic test rate, not a price" or "synthetic test limit, not a
  budget". The recorded usage in `tests/fixtures/cost/prometheus_responses.json` covers
  synthetic sessions: 40/10 per day and 900/300 per month by profile. Nothing on a serving path
  reads either file. They exist so the arithmetic, the verdicts, the alert expressions and the
  renderer have something to multiply.
- The one window constant is 30 d for a month and 1 d for a day, both rolling. They are stated
  in the price file header.

No figure reaches `docs/regulatory/`, `docs/legal/`, patient or clinician copy (inv 20).

## 6. The acceptance, clause by clause

> **"per-session cost by component/vendor/profile"**: **met as a mechanism.**

The payload has one row per component and profile, with `vendor` and `attribution`, a
per-vendor total per profile, and a per-profile total, for a 1 d and a 30 d window. The renderer
paints a figure as one money atom (`useGrouping:false`, NBSP, inv 22) and a quantity as one
quantity-and-unit atom. `tests/admin/test_cost_module.py` proves the arithmetic on recorded
responses: 48 000 characters over 40 `residency_tr` sessions at the synthetic rate of 2 gives
2 400 per session. The committed state carries no money at all
(`test_the_committed_state_carries_no_money_at_all`,
`e2e/cost.spec.ts › carries no money anywhere on the page`).

> **"budgets"**: **met as a mechanism; every limit is unset.**

Per vendor (`self-hosted`, `elevenlabs`, `aws`) and for all vendors, per day and per month, the
schema refuses a vendor without both periods. The verdicts are `within`, `exceeded`,
`not_evaluable` and `not_configured`, with the soundness rule above. The renderer refuses
`within` over a bound and any verdict with no limit.

> **"alerts"**: **met.**

The rules are generated from the price file and drift-gated. Only catalogued series appear
(`catalog.unknown_references(expr) == ∅` over both configs). They are mounted and loaded by the
obs Prometheus, and each budget's alert names are carried in the console payload.

**Status: `awaiting-human`**, on the owner's rates and limits (HB-O8 (b), with the day/month
spend budgets as the second half of that ask; docs/backlog/blockers.md). No HB id was minted.

## 8. Findings reported rather than fixed

**F1 — no `GET /admin/cost` route yet.** The composite is produced by
`intake_platform.cost.compose_cost_payload`, and its body is recorded by
`scripts/dev/record_cost_fixture.py`. That is the same posture the security, experience, safety
and protocols modules shipped in. Serving it needs an `ADMIN_ROUTES` row and a Prometheus reader.
INT-351 (PR #562) adds both the rendered console shell and `ops_prom`. Wiring the route there,
rather than here, avoids a second Prometheus client and a conflicting edit to the route table.

**F2 — three components have no catalogued usage series on main** (extraction tokens, clip
synthesis, host hours). The table in §4 shows each one, and none was minted. Clip synthesis
needs a characters-vs-clips decision before any series is attached.

**F3 — `cost.per_session` stays `planned`.** The console computes per-session cost at read
time. A per-session *gauge* would need the runtime to hold a price, which INT-101 declined for
good reason, so the catalogue row's description now says so.
