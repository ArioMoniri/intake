# INT-337 — Motion: dialogue transients wired to the arbiter

**Epic:** E08-mobile · **Phase:** P2 · **Sec-Row:** intake-voice · **GitHub:** #536 · **Labels:** area:mobile
**Spec:** `docs/design/motion/motion-verification.md` §4 MOTION-8; `intake-voice-motion-spec.md` C1, C3–C10, §3.3 and §3.4; ADR-033 §2 (PR #571, **Proposed**).
**Branch:** `int-337-dialogue-transients`. It is stacked on `int-336-presence-orb` (#716), because it wires the arbiter's work-chip decision into the orb's ring, which INT-336 adds. The PR targets `main` and shows INT-336's commits until #716 merges.

## Acceptance (verbatim, docs/backlog/issues.yaml)
1. "content enters with a 120 ms opacity-only fade at delay 0 and is never deferred; the patient's own typed message renders instantly; no frame shows two questions"
2. "transcript reveal and work chips are commit-window classes, deferred while listening or composing and flushed at the next window; chip selection and confirm commits add no motion"

**Row status: `done`.** Both lines are pure policy plus source wiring, and both are tested here. No line needs a device measurement.

## Change
- **Pure policy** in `src/lib/motion/transients.ts` (no React Native, Reanimated or clock).
  - `dialogueScene(state, { composerFocused, chipsOpen, strip })` describes what the dialogue shows, as far as motion is concerned:
    - one entry per stream entry, under a key that stays stable when another entry is removed;
    - the confirm chip group, as one element;
    - whether work is in flight;
    - the strip's count;
    - whether the arbiter's commit window is open.
  - `stepDialogueMotion` diffs two scenes and asks the INT-334 arbiter about every new element. Each element type gets a fixed class:
    - agent bubbles and the chip group are `content`, which is never deferred;
    - the patient's typed message, and the bubble opened at the microphone, render at once, outside the arbiter;
    - a transcript reveal is a `railCommit`, keyed to the new text node only;
    - the work indicator is a `workChip`;
    - the strip's count waits for the window and swaps instantly, so it never takes the animation slot.
  - Exits are instant. A deferred commit for an element that is gone is invalidated and never shown.
  - `fadeOf` grants a fade only to the arbiter's running element, and never under reduce-motion. `nodeKey` remounts a snapped element at its end state.
- **Device half:** `src/lib/motion/native/useDialogueMotion.ts`.
  - It steps the reducer in the render that changes the scene (React's derived-state pattern), so a decision lands in the same commit as its element.
  - It reports a fade's end with a timer set to the fade the policy granted. A Reanimated callback would need `runOnJS`, which INT-335 forbids.
  - The timer frees the arbiter's slot and nothing else. BK13 classifies it in `NOT_A_TIME_LIMIT`.
- **Dialogue** (`src/components/DialogueIntake.tsx`):
  - The stream sits in `LayoutAnimationConfig skipEntering`, so history does not fade in.
  - Every entry sits in an `Animated.View` whose `entering` is `enteringFor(fadeOf(…))`.
  - A transcript stays on its "Yazıya dökülüyor…" line until its reveal is applied, and then only the text node fades.
  - The confirm chips enter as one group.
  - The composer's focus closes the commit window.
  - The strip shows the arbiter's count.
  - The orb's ring is the work chip: `working` comes from the reducer, and `workFades` is the arbiter's grant.
- **Orb** (`src/components/PresenceOrb.tsx`): a `workFades` prop. The ring switches instantly unless the arbiter granted it the fade. Removal and snaps are therefore instant, and nothing fades out.
- No copy changed. `src/lib/dialogue/delivery.ts` (INT-414) and the i18n files are untouched.

## Not in this PR
- **The orb holding its loop during a transient** (ARB-2, the arbiter's `ambient: "held"`). The arbiter computes it, and INT-334's tests pin it. It is not in this row's acceptance, and resuming a Reanimated loop from its held value needs its own device check. The orb's loops keep running beside a transient, which the intake spec's P0 allows ("one transient at a time, alongside the ambient orb").
- **Labelled work chips** (02 §5.2's fixed table). The dev dialogue has no such UI, and new copy belongs with the i18n owner. The orb's ring is the dialogue's work chip, and it follows the work-chip rules.
