## The determination: a survey invitation is a **commercial** message

This is the decision the issue turns on, so it is stated first and it is not a formality.

**6563 m.2/1(c)** defines a *ticari elektronik ileti* as data, voice or image content sent
electronically **ticari amaçlarla** — the test is the sender's purpose, not whether the
text sells something. **The Yönetmelik (RG 29417, 15.07.2015) m.6** is where the
consent-free messages live: those concerning the **change, use or maintenance** of a good
or service the recipient asked for, and notifications of a continuing subscription,
membership or partnership, collection, debt reminder, information update, purchase and
delivery **or similar situations**, plus statutory information duties — and it closes by
forbidding those messages from promoting or advertising any good or service.

An invitation to rate us is none of those:

- it does not concern the change, use or maintenance of the care the patient received;
- it notifies them of nothing about their own transaction — the service they asked for is
  complete and correct whether or not they answer;
- it exists because **we** want the answer. NPS is a loyalty and advocacy measure, and the
  question it asks is literally whether the recipient would recommend us, which sits on
  the promotional edge of the carve-out rather than in the safe middle of it.

Contrast INT-141's reminders, which genuinely are transactional: they tell the patient
about the preparation for a visit **they** booked, and without them the service they asked
for does not happen properly. The distinction is real, and a survey falls on the other
side of it.

**So the invitation needs an İYS-registered consent, and no İYS registry exists in this
tree.** `UnconfiguredIysRegistry` refuses every lookup and `IysConsentRecord` is abstract
with no concrete subclass outside tests (INT-141, locks 2 and 3). **The patient-facing NPS
invitation therefore cannot be sent at all today, and that is what ships.**

Three notes on the edges of the determination, each written down rather than smoothed
over:

1. **The classification that ships is the conservative one.** Reading a survey as
   commercial can only make the product send *less*. If counsel later reads m.6's *benzeri
   durumlar* more widely, relaxing it is a reviewed change to one constant; the reverse
   mistake is unconsented marketing to patients.
2. **The tacir/esnaf question is not answered.** The Yönetmelik treats messages to
   merchants differently, and a clinician is a professional recipient — but "which of our
   clinician recipients is a *tacir* or an *esnaf*" is a fact about each contract, not
   about this code. Both survey kinds are classified identically, which is the
   under-sending side.
3. **An in-product prompt is not an *ileti* at all.** `InvitationChannel.IN_PRODUCT` is a
   card rendered to someone already signed in: nothing is transmitted to a contact
   address, no lock screen lights up. 6563 does not engage — but the `satisfaction_prompts`
   safety flag still does, so "6563 does not engage" is not "anything goes".

The repo had already taken this position and I did not have to invent it:
`satisfaction_prompts` has sat in `SAFETY_FLAG_DEFAULTS` at `False` since INT-023 with the
comment *"İYS posture (inv. 18): no prompt without consent"*. INT-242 is its first caller.

**The one refactor, and why.** The acceptance says the prompt honours quiet hours and
opt-outs, both of which INT-141 already decides. A survey scheduler with its own copy of
"is it quiet now?" drifts from the dispatcher on the first edit, and the person who finds
out is the one being woken. So `ReminderDispatcher._decide`'s quiet-hours arm moved into
`quiet_verdict()` and both callers use it. Behaviour is unchanged — INT-141's whole suite
passes untouched — and `test_the_planner_and_the_reminder_dispatcher_never_disagree`
forces the two to agree across 48 combinations of opt-out × baseline × patient window ×
hour. Opt-out stayed a plain attribute read rather than joining the shared verdict,
because the two callers read it at different points in their own order of refusal.

**Order of refusal**, strongest first, pinned by `TestOrderOfRefusal` which shuts every
lock at once and then removes them one at a time:

1. **Opt-out** — the recipient's own refusal, named first even when something else would
   also have stopped the send.
2. **İYS** — law before configuration.
3. **`satisfaction_prompts`** — the safety flag, shut.
4. **The schedule** — refuses when the unsupplied first-prompt value is needed.
5. **Quiet hours** — INT-141's verdict, with an unset baseline refusing.

**Nothing is sent, by five independent constructions**: the flag is shut; the İYS gate
refuses; the schedule value is unset; `ReminderKind` has no survey member so the
transactional dispatcher cannot carry an invitation; and `TEMPLATES` has no survey
template, so there is no copy to render even with every gate open. `InvitationStatus.ISSUED`
is a statement about eligibility, never a delivery — the planner does not send.

## Values refused, and the asks

Nothing invented. Two figures are stated in the tree and used; everything else is absent
and stays absent.

**Used, because stated:** `nps → monthly` and `clinician_pulse → quarterly`, from the
acceptance line above and from files/08 §4 group F (`nps.monthly`; `clinician.pulse`
"quarterly 1-5"). A period is a **calendar** span, not a day count: 31 January + 1 month
is 28 February, and three of those make a quarter. Thirty and ninety days are different,
invented cadences that drift a week a year.

**Refused:**

| value | why it is not here | state today |
|---|---|---|
| **First-prompt delay** — how long after a recipient becomes eligible the *first* invitation falls due | No file states it. It is the one value the mechanism genuinely cannot derive: a recipient with no prompt history has no period to advance from. Once prompted once, the stated period answers "when next" with no further value, which is why the gap is exactly one field wide. | `SurveyPolicy.first_prompt_after` ships `None` and `due()` returns `REFUSED_FIRST_PROMPT_DELAY_UNSET`; the planner reports `REFUSED_SCHEDULE_UNSET`. |
| **What makes a recipient eligible** — account created? first session completed? tenant go-live? | The other half of the same question: it decides `SurveyRecipient.eligible_since`, and the three candidates give materially different first-prompt dates. | The field is the caller's to supply; nothing in the tree supplies it, because nothing calls the planner in production yet. |
| **Sampling rate** | No file states one, and "monthly NPS prompt" reads as a per-recipient cadence. Adding an unset sampling knob that always refuses would be theatre; adding a set one would be inventing a rate. | **Not a concept in this package.** `test_the_package_declares_no_sampling_rate_or_cool_off` keeps it that way, with a near-miss control. |
| **Cross-survey cool-off** | Same. And within this scheduler there is nothing to collide with: the two kinds have different audiences, and CSAT (INT-240) is session-triggered rather than scheduled. The real open question is CSAT-then-NPS at the same patient. | Not a concept here; scanned for. |
| **Send hour / day-of-period anchor** | Quiet hours are INT-141's, and INT-141 ships no baseline window because no file names an hour. Naming a send hour here would route around that refusal. | No `HH:MM` literal exists in the package; an unset baseline refuses. |
| **Invitation copy** | Patient-facing text is ≤ B1 / PolicyGuard territory and no file states an invitation body. | `TEMPLATES` has no survey template; `test_there_is_no_survey_template_to_render` pins it. |
| **An invitation-history table** | Persisting "who was invited when" is a new category of personal data (CLAUDE.md stop-and-ask) — and 6563 keeps **sender records**, whose shape depends on the İYS integration nobody has specified. Building the table now would be inventing that shape for rows that cannot lawfully exist yet. | No migration. `SurveyRecipient.last_prompted_at` is an argument; the store lands with the İYS adapter, and is reviewed then. |

## Boundaries this issue did not close

- **Nothing calls the planner in production.** There is no scheduler process:
  `services/workers` does not exist yet, and a periodic job that can only ever record
  `blocked_iys_consent_absent` would be a process built to do nothing. The planner is a
  pure decision the job wires up when there is something it may lawfully send.
- **No new route.** The acceptance names none; INT-240 built the two ingest routes and
  deliberately left `clinician_pulse` without one; files/08 §5's "configure survey cadence"
  is an admin-console action that lands with the console issues (INT-180 onward). So
  `unguarded_data_routes` is untouched and no new consent scope was declared — the single
  `ensure_consent` path has nothing new to enforce here.
