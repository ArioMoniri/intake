**The open clinical question, answered.** A blanket nil-by-mouth line does conflict with
dispatcher-led and guideline care: the first treatment of a suspected acute coronary syndrome
is aspirin, chewed or dissolved by mouth while help is awaited (first aid, ERC 2025; NHS), which
NICE CG95 has a clinician offer as soon as possible and records when given before arrival at
hospital, and the 999/112 call handler gives that advice. No retrieved
source supports nil by mouth for suspected ACS. The line goes, and nothing takes its place.

New verbatim snapshots: `NHS-HEART-ATTACK-2026.txt`, `RCUK-ERC-FIRST-AID-2025.txt`,
`NICE-CG95-B.txt` (section 1.2.3, beside the existing CG95 excerpt), `EENA-SILENT-CALLS-2020.txt`.
The ERC 2025 First Aid article returned HTTP 403 at both publisher URLs, so the Resuscitation
Council UK guideline that reproduces its recommendations is the snapshot. Every quote is checked
verbatim by the register loader. Both changed lines pass the shipped Turkish PolicyGuard
(`policy_guard_for(locale="tr", profile=RESIDENCY_TR)`): 0 violations, no digits, `siz`.
