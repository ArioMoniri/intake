# Evidence — INT-188 · Flags module (Unleash) + 4-eyes on safety flags

- **Epic/phase:** E12-admin · P1 · `sec_row: admin-console`
- **Depends on:** INT-023 (flag facade + Unleash provider), INT-180 (admin shell + four-eyes primitives)
- **Acceptance, verbatim:** "change reasons mandatory; safety flags list incl. device_functions, s2s, learning-apply"
- **Named test:** `tests/admin/test_flag_governance.py`

## What was built, and the decision that shapes it

The write side of `intake_platform.flags`. INT-023 built the read side — the
six safety flags read `False` and cannot be defaulted open, and the Unleash
provider falls closed. INT-188 is where a change *originates*, as a pure-rule
module `intake_platform.admin.flag_governance` (no chain, no metric — the audit
row and the self-approval counter belong to `services/api/admin`, exactly as
`break_glass.py` leaves them).

**The one decision:** INT-188's one-line acceptance predates the 25 Aug
admin-console security audit (files/08 §0b), and where they meet the audit is
the authority (CLAUDE.md read-order: files/00 wins). So "4-eyes on safety
flags" is implemented as the audit resolved it, not as the 2024 backlog line
imagined it:

- **Safety switches do not live in the generic (Unleash) flag store at all**
  (invariant 34 `device_flag_gate_guard`, its flag-store-scan half; §0b F1/F2,
  §3, §8.2). `guard_generic_flag_write` / `scan_generic_store` are that scan;
  `FlagGovernor.set_operational` refuses a safety key rather than writing one.
  A safety flag is decided only by the tier-3 ceremony.
- **Enabling a safety flag is the dangerous direction and takes two keys.**
  `SAFETY_FLAG_ENABLE` (registered in `DANGEROUS_ACTIONS`, tier 3, `clinical_lead
  + qms_owner`-shaped approver set, `approver_must_differ_org`) opens the
  four-eyes ceremony through the INT-180 `FourEyesService`; the flip lands only
  after a **distinct human, credential and session, from a distinct
  organisation, with a fresh single-use assertion** approves. The near-miss —
  **a safety flag enabled by one person is refused** — is
  `test_one_person_cannot_enable_a_safety_flag` (self-approval raises, the flag
  stays `False`).
- **Pulling a safety flag to its safe (OFF) state takes one key**
  (`SAFETY_FLAG_DISABLE_ROLES`), because a second approver on the safe direction
  is an availability control on a safety control (§0c F17). This is the
  asymmetry the `DangerousAction` tier-1 constructor already enforces elsewhere.

### Invariants honoured (asserted, not described)

- **Inv 12 (proposal-only / a flag never touches clinical semantics):**
  `CLINICAL_SEMANTIC_SURFACES` = {red_flag_levels, instruments, consent_texts,
  talker_tool_list, policyguard, profile_routing}; `guard_flag_key` refuses any
  of them as a flag key, and the governed set is disjoint from them
  (`test_safety_flags_are_disjoint_from_clinical_surfaces`). `learning_apply`
  even ON only gates whether a learning *proposal* may be applied — never a
  clinical value.
- **Inv 14 (four-eyes second authoriser is a distinct human + authenticator):**
  reuses the INT-180 `FourEyesService`, whose distinctness checks (account,
  WebAuthn credential, session, org at T3, single-use assertion) are already
  tested. Change reasons are a closed `FlagChangeReason` class + mandatory free
  text, enforced at the builder AND at the write.
- **Inv 6 (no PHI/PII in logs/labels):** every refusal carries a stable
  `flag_*` code, never the submitted text (`test_a_refusal_never_echoes_the_
  submitted_text`); the pending-action evidence a second pair of eyes reads is
  id-shaped only (`{"flag": ..., "enabled": "true"}`), never the reason text.
- **Inv 17 (no placeholders / fail-closed):** `no_placeholders` green; the
  safety store defaults all-`False` and the generic store cannot hold a safety
  key — the fail-closed state, not a stub.

## Provider-agnostic / no live Unleash

The read-side `UnleashProvider` (INT-023) is reached only behind
`INTAKE_UNLEASH_URL` and is exercised with `httpx.MockTransport` fixtures — no
test or CI touches a live Unleash server. INT-188 adds no network path: the
governor is in-process rule over two in-memory stores. The "flag proxy" that
mirrors an operational Unleash write into `admin_audit.v1` (files/08 §3) is the
governor + the `services/api/admin` audit seam; wiring a live Unleash **admin**
API write is a later surface and would arrive behind the same config flag and
recorded fixtures.

## PHI egress enumeration

No new outbound call. The module makes no I/O at all (no HTTP, no DB, no file,
no logger). The read-side Unleash call is unchanged and carries no session
context (INT-023's `test_ctx_is_never_transmitted_to_unleash`).
