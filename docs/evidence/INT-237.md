# Evidence — INT-237 · Dev-lane TTS delivery shaping

## Scope

Owner-reported defect against the dev-lane spoken dialogue ("tts is so
awkward"). No backlog row: this is a defect report, not a planned issue.

The change is confined to the mobile TTS seam —
`apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/speechShaping.ts` (new),
`apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/tts.ts` and the one line in
`apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/clips.ts` that binds the clip store to it.
No file on the GPU box was modified.

**Invariant 10** — scripted text is spoken verbatim. Shaping is
punctuation-neutral on words: chunks are exact substrings of the
whitespace-collapsed input, in order, covering it completely; the only
addition is a terminal mark on a chunk's spoken form. Pinned in
`speechShaping.test.ts` over every string in the Turkish catalog.

**Invariant 22** — no numeral reaches any TTS. `assertSpeakableTr` is
unchanged and still throws before any request leaves the app; the daemon's
independent `digits_forbidden` check is untouched. A test pins that shaping
introduces no digit into a chunk that had none.

**Invariant 20** — everything below is a measurement with a date and a named
corpus. Nothing here is a claim about the product.

## What was measured

- **Corpus**: 16 probes. Twelve are the agent's composed question and
  confirm turns, assembled by `speech.ts` from verbatim
  `apps/mobile/src/lib/i18n/tr.ts` catalog strings; four are the RED-register
  bare imperative plurals recorded in CLAUDE.md and `files/00_STATE.md` §2
  (`Tutun`, `Arayın`, `Bakmayın`) individually and as one string. No RED
  action script `text_tr` exists in the repo yet — the compiled packs are
  INT-096 — so the imperatives are the only committed RED-register material.
- **Date**: 29 Aug 2026.
- **Lane**: FreyaTTS daemon, `127.0.0.1:8803`, `device=cuda`,
  `normalizer=disabled`; model `freyavoice/Freya-TTS`
  @ `d124e07493615208f58bdd21d432736849ee4230`, inference code pinned
  @ `146d36c1cb6660646be57d31339db4eed9315de3` (files/03 §4.9.1 row 12).
- **Harness**: `/data/intake/work/tts_eval/tts_measure.py` (baseline) and
  `tts_shaped.py` (shaped reference implementation of the client seam).
  WAVs under `/data/intake/work/tts_eval/{baseline,shaped}/`.
- **Method**: 10 ms frames; a frame under −45 dBFS is silence; a silent run
  ≥ 60 ms is a pause. Rate is non-space characters per second of speech,
  excluding leading and trailing silence.

Four findings, in order of how much of the complaint they explain.

**F1 · The voice is band-limited to ~3.1–3.4 kHz.** The 99 % spectral
rolloff never exceeds 3445 Hz on any probe; energy above 4 kHz is between
0.006 % and 0.7 % of the total, and above 8 kHz it is effectively zero. The
container is 48 kHz but the content is narrowband. This matches the trap
recorded in files/03 §4.9.1 row 12 — "one telephony-band voice (Leyla)".
Turkish `ş s ç z f` carry their distinguishing energy between 4 and 8 kHz,
and none of it is present. **Not addressable at this seam** — see Verdict.

**F2 · Sentence boundaries are not distinguished from commas.** The
daemon's `_clauses` splitter concatenates every clause with the same fixed
120 ms gap (`pipeline.py::_synth`). On b06 the boundary after the question
mark measured **90 ms** — identical to a comma. Across the corpus the
largest internal gap ranges 90–540 ms with no relation to whether the
boundary is a comma or a full stop.

**F3 · The duration head allocates time erratically.** Frames are 25 Hz
(`vae.py`), and `_synth_one` derives the frame count from a **mean-pooled**
text embedding — `pooled = (te * cmask).sum(1) / cmask.sum(1)` — so its
prediction tracks average character identity, not length or phonetic
content. Measured frames-per-character falls monotonically with length
(5 chars → 5.00, 8 → 3.25, 13 → 2.38, 24 → 1.67): short text is drawled,
long text is rushed. Worse, it is not even a function of length — two
single-chunk strings of 31 and 33 non-space characters measured **3.32 and
1.73 frames/char, a 1.9× rate difference**
(`Seçenekler: Sağ, Sol veya İki taraf.` vs
`Seçenekleri ekranda görebilirsiniz.`). **Not correctable at this seam.**

**F4 · Ragged turn edges.** Leading silence spans 30–150 ms and trailing
0–250 ms. Two RED clips (r01, r02) end with **zero** trailing silence — a
hard cut from speech to nothing.

The most consequential single number is r04: the three RED imperatives sent
as one string are delivered at **15.38 c/s**, against 6.41 / 6.67 / 8.79
c/s for the same three words sent separately — the same words 2.4× faster,
separated by 90 ms, because the duration head under-allocates the longer
string. Delivery of a RED action script is a safety-relevant property, not
a matter of taste.

Level is not implicated: peak 0.50–0.89, RMS −22.7 to −25.9 dBFS, **zero
clipped samples** anywhere in the corpus.

What moved, and what did not:

- **Leading silence 30–150 ms → 10–20 ms**; spread 120 ms → 10 ms.
- **Trailing silence 0–250 ms → 0–30 ms**; spread 250 ms → 30 ms. Both
  edges now carry an 8 ms fade, so the two zero-trail RED clips no longer
  cut hard.
- **Sentence boundaries now land at 300–320 ms** on every multi-sentence
  probe, against 90–540 ms before. b06 moved 90 → 320 ms.
- **r04 (RED triplet) 15.38 → 6.87 c/s**, with 320 ms between each
  imperative: the three now match the rate of the same words sent
  individually (5.88 / 7.06 / 9.30 c/s).
- **Median duration change +0.21 s per turn.** Two probes cost more: b05
  +1.32 s and r04 +1.62 s. r04 is the intended RED change. b05 is not a gap
  cost — it is F3: split out on its own, the clause
  `Seçenekler: Sağ, Sol veya İki taraf.` drew 103 frames where the whole
  string had drawn ~100, and its rate fell to 8.33 c/s.
- **Speaking rate is not improved and was not expected to be.** The spread
  across composed questions is 10.38–14.71 c/s before and 8.33–13.26 c/s
  after. Rate is set by the duration head (F3) and no client-side chunking
  reaches it. b03's 610 ms max gap is the daemon's own, inside the 80-char
  options clause, not one of ours.

## Server-side knobs: inventory, and why none was changed

`POST /synthesize` accepts `{"text"}` and nothing else — the request model
carries no delivery parameter. Reading the pinned pipeline, the parameters
that exist are internal:

| parameter | where | why it was not used |
|---|---|---|
| `steps` (32) | `_synth` | sampler step count, not a delivery control |
| `seed` (`LEYLA_SEED`) | `_synth` | the seed *is* the speaker — `x0` is the voice, and the model has no speaker conditioning. Changing it changes who is talking, breaking the one-voice-per-session identity requirement (files/03 §4.9.1 row 11) |
| `t_floor` (8) | constructor | a floor only; the predicted `T` binds on every probe measured, so raising it changes nothing until it exceeds the prediction |
| `max_words` (11) | constructor | clause-chunk width; the sentence gap is what is missing, and that is expressible client-side |
| inter-clause gap (0.12 s) | `_synth`, hardcoded | correct *as a clause gap*. With one sentence per request it now applies only within a sentence, which is where 120 ms belongs |

There is no rate or duration-scale parameter. The only lever on rate is
`T`, which is computed inside `_synth_one` from the duration head; scaling
it would move the flow-matching model off the operating point it was
trained at, on a model files/03 §4.9.1 already designates as the
low-footprint fallback. **No file under `/data/intake` was modified, so no
backup was needed.** The added files are read-only additions under
`/data/intake/work/tts_eval/`.

## Verdict on the voice model

**The dominant cause is the model, not the delivery.** F1 and F3 are
architectural properties of the pinned 183M artefact:

- a ~3.1–3.4 kHz band, which no client-side processing can widen, and which
  removes the spectral region Turkish sibilants and fricatives live in;
- a duration head that regresses frame count from a mean-pooled text
  embedding, giving a 1.9× rate difference between two strings of the same
  length and a 2.4× difference on the RED triplet.

Both are recorded in files/03 §4.9.1 row 12 as known traps of this row, and
that row is the **low-footprint / CPU fallback** — not the designated
production path. The cached-clip and dynamic paths are rows 10 and 11,
`openbmb/VoxCPM2`, the one model in the survey where Turkish was evaluated
rather than listed; row 13 `ResembleAI/chatterbox` is the held third
option.

The shaping in this change is worth having on its own terms — the sentence
gap, the trimmed edges and above all the RED delivery are real, measured
and independent of which model sits behind the lane, and they carry over to
VoxCPM2 unchanged. But it does not make the dev lane sound like the
production tier, and it was not going to.

**Path forward is blocked on a human, and this change does not unblock
it.** VoxCPM2 and chatterbox are not deployed here: their `LICENSE` blobs
are not yet verified from source, and invariant 19 requires verification
from the raw blob with a recorded `licence_verified_at` in the model
registry before either may be pulled onto the PHI path. That verification
is a human action; it is not currently carried as a row in
`files/HUMAN_BLOCKERS.md` and should be added before either row is
scheduled. Marked `awaiting-human`; no substitute was deployed and no
licence status was assumed.
