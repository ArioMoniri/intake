## 4 · Clause 1 — "excludes wellbeing signals", as an allow-list

### Why an allow-list and not a deny-list

INT-116's acceptance says the wellbeing outputs are *"never in patient summary"*, and files/02
§5.6 says the same in product terms: the patient sees plain-language check-in results, **"never
scores and never labels"**. A deny-list expresses that as a list of things to remember. It is
correct on the day it is written and wrong on the day somebody adds the next signal, because a
new field is admitted by default and nothing fails.

So the rule runs the other way, at two independent levels:

1. **Sections.** `PATIENT_VISIBLE_SECTIONS` (`chief_complaint`, `hpi`, `medications`,
   `allergies`) and `CLINICIAN_ONLY_SECTIONS` (everything else, `screening` and
   `konusma_ozellikleri` named first) must **partition** `ATTRIBUTABLE_SECTIONS` exactly.
   `section_partition_error()` is evaluated **at import**, and `summary.py` raises
   `SectionPartitionError` if a note section is classified neither way — so a new note section is
   a build failure across the whole API, not a silent leak on the first note that carries one.
2. **Fields.** `PATIENT_VISIBLE_FIELDS` names 18 normalised field references. Matching is on the
   **whole** normalised reference, never a prefix, and it reaches inside list items: a medication
   line is rebuilt from `medications[].name` and `medications[].dose`, so the composer's
   clinician shorthand (`atc`, `adherence`, `source`, `reconciliation`) never crosses.

`ComposedSummary.withheld_field_refs` records what was kept back, so the exclusion is auditable
rather than invisible. On the `red_full` fixture it holds 28 references including all four
instrument scores and all seven `konusma_ozellikleri` measures.

### A newly added wellbeing field, with nothing edited

`TestANewSignalIsExcludedWithoutEditingTheRule` proves it twice:

- **introspectively** — every name in `ScreeningBlock.model_fields` is asserted outside
  `PATIENT_VISIBLE_FIELDS`, which covers fields that do not exist yet: whatever `ScreeningBlock`
  grows, the allow-list does not grow with it;
- **end to end** — a `NoteFact(section="screening", field_ref="screening.who5", …)` is injected
  into a composed note exactly as the composer would emit one for a new field, and the built
  summary withholds it and neither surface contains its text. No exclusion rule is edited to make
  that happen.

The same shape covers list items: `test_a_new_item_field_is_outside_the_allow_list_by_default`
walks `MedicationItem.model_fields` and `AllergyItem.model_fields` and asserts the allow-list
admits exactly the two patient-facing ones from each.

### Near-miss negative controls

| control | why it is a near miss | expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hpi.negatives[0]` beside `hpi.narrative` | the two share the five-character prefix `hpi.n`; the first is an elicited negative in the patient's own words, the second is model-composed prose about the patient | negative **admitted**, narrative **withheld** — a prefix match gets exactly one of them wrong |
| a chief complaint reading *"kendimi yorgun ve mutsuz hissediyorum"* | it reads like a PHQ item, but it is the patient's own presenting complaint arriving through a patient-facing field | **survives verbatim** — dropping it would be the allow-list over-firing on the one sentence the patient came to say |
| `suggested_focus_questions` beside `questions_for_doctor` | `PatientSummary` has a questions field the clinician-facing focus questions would slot straight into | **withheld**; the patient's own questions come from the wrap-up slot `open_questions` (`packages/protocols/base/intake.yaml`) and arrive as an attributed argument |

### The exclusion survives rendering

Two surfaces are emitted and both are checked: `render_patient_summary()` (text) and
`PatientSummary.model_dump_json()` (the wire form). No PDF or HTML surface is added by this
issue, so there is none to check — `TestExclusionSurvivesRendering` also asserts that **every
non-blank line of the render is either a catalogue value or a value the allow-list admitted**,
which is what makes "there is no second door" a checked property rather than a claim: the
renderer holds only the `PatientSummary` and cannot re-read the note.

## 5 · Clause 2 — the readability metric, and what it may not claim

### The decision

**No Turkish readability formula is used, because none is committed here and none is named in
`files/`.** Flesch-Kincaid, SMOG and their relatives are English formulas with English constants
fitted to English syllable and clause statistics. Turkish is agglutinative — one word carries what
English spreads over four — so syllables-per-word runs high and words-per-sentence runs low for
text of the same difficulty, and the English constants are not about this language. Running one
anyway and reporting the output as a CEFR B1 level would be a **fabricated measurement presented
as a property of the product**, which CLAUDE.md invariant 20 forbids outright, and it would
require inventing a threshold, which HUMAN_BLOCKERS.md rule 1 forbids outright.

Turkish formulas do exist in the literature. **None of them is in this repository, and none is
cited in any blueprint file** (`grep -in "readab|okunab"` over `files/` returns nothing), so
naming one here would mean transcribing a formula and a cut-off from memory — the same failure
mode as an invented clinical threshold, and with no diff that makes it look wrong.

So the gate is split:

**(a) Structural checks — implemented, and they block a release.** Each is a countable fact about
the text, not an estimate of a reading level:

| check | operationalisation | why it is defensible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SENTENCE_LENGTH` | words per sentence ≤ `B1_MAX_WORDS_PER_SENTENCE` = 15 | **not chosen here.** It is the number the runtime PolicyGuard already enforces at the microphone (`intake_voice_runtime.talker.policy_guard.B1_MAX_WORDS_PER_SENTENCE`). `services/api` does not depend on `services/voice-runtime`, so the constant is mirrored rather than imported, and `test_the_sentence_bound_is_the_runtime_bound` pins the two equal so they cannot drift into two answers to the same question |
| `ONE_IDEA` | no sentence-internal `;`, at most one clause-joining coordinator from a closed set | one coordinator joins two nouns and is ordinary Turkish (*"Ateş ve öksürük"* passes); two of them is two clauses wearing one full stop |
| `REGISTER_SEN` | the `sen` pronoun in every case, plus the free interrogative clitic | the unambiguous core of PolicyGuard's morphological register test — no other Turkish word is any of these tokens, so it is a **subset by construction** and cannot disagree with the guard in the direction that matters. *"Notunuzu gördünüz mü?"* passes, which is the `siz`-prefix near miss |
| `JARGON` | a term from the closed medical vocabulary appearing **without a gloss**; a parenthesis immediately after clears it | CLAUDE.md says *"no medical jargon without explanation"*, not "no medical jargon". `test_every_jargon_term_is_jargon_to_policyguard_too` asserts every term here is jargon to the runtime guard as well, so the screen and the microphone share one vocabulary |
| `INSTRUCTIONAL_FORM` | in prose, a second-person-plural imperative must take `-iniz`; **inside RED the rule inverts** and the bare imperative plural is required | CLAUDE.md's register rule, including its own inversion: *"Invert inside RED, where the bare imperative plural (Tutun · Arayın · Bakmayın) is correct because ambiguity is the harm."* `red_register` is a property of the text unit with no default, because a default here would be chosen once by an engineer for every future string |

Detection is by **closed stem inventory as positive evidence**, following the INT-248 lesson the
guard next door already paid for: `al`, `aç`, `iç`, `yaz`, bare `ara`, `sor` and `gel` are
deliberately **absent** from the imperative inventory because their imperative forms are also
everyday Turkish nouns — *alın* is a forehead, *sorun* is a problem, *gelin* is a bride. Missing
one instructional sentence is a gap; flagging a correct one blocks the release of a correct
summary, and precision is the safety property here.

**(b) Scored metrics — the mechanism, wired and empty.** `SCORED_METRICS` is an empty tuple.
`ReadabilityMetric` refuses construction without a name and a `formula_source` naming the
committed artefact its formula is read from — so a row cannot be added without provenance, the
same discipline a red-flag rule carries. `ReadabilityReport.asserts_b1_level` is therefore
`False` and `structural_only` is `True`: **the gate reports structure and claims no reading
level.** `test_no_scored_readability_metric_is_committed` and `test_the_report_claims_no_b1_level`
pin that.

### Invariant 20 in the artefacts

- No readability, accuracy or performance figure appears in any patient-facing string, in
  `docs/regulatory/` or in `docs/legal/` — this issue writes nothing into either tree.
- `test_no_readability_figure_appears_in_the_summary_surfaces` runs
  `intake_platform.legal.claims.scan_text` over both emitted surfaces; INT-084's
  `test_the_note_package_never_names_a_performance_metric` already scans the whole note package
  and covers the two new modules automatically.
- The only measurements recorded anywhere are in this file, dated, and described as measurements:
  **48 tests, 173 in `tests/engine`, one run on 31 Aug 2026 against the synthetic fixtures of
  `tests/engine/note_fixtures.py`** — a named corpus of three synthetic scenarios, not a patient
  corpus and not a claim about the product.

### The one thing the gate deliberately will not do

`ReadabilityReport.gate_passes` is decided by **authored copy alone**. A finding on the patient's
own reproduced words is recorded with `origin=QUOTED` and never fails the build, because
paraphrasing a patient's sentence back at them to satisfy a word count would break the promise
files/02 §5.2 makes with the rail — the patient watches *their* words become structure — and
would put a model between the patient and their own account.
`test_the_patients_own_words_are_reported_and_never_rewritten` pins both halves: the finding is
reported, the sentence is rendered unchanged, and the build passes.

## 8 · Scope not taken, and why

- **`PatientSummary.bring_list` and `questions_for_doctor` are arguments, not derivations.** The
  bring list is resolved protocol-pack copy and the patient's questions are the wrap-up slot
  `open_questions`; neither exists on `PreVisitNote`, and inventing a derivation for either would
  have meant deriving patient-facing content from clinician-facing fields — exactly the wiring
  `test_the_clinician_focus_questions_are_not_the_patients_questions` exists to forbid. They are
  accepted as inputs (questions attributed and gated as quoted; the bring list gated as authored)
  and default to empty.
- **No PDF or HTML surface.** The acceptance names neither, and a surface that is not emitted is
  a surface the exclusion does not have to be re-proved on. Both surfaces this issue does emit are
  checked.
- **`docs/PROGRESS.md`, `docs/backlog/**` and `files/**` were not updated** — outside the file
  fence for this issue. No git command was run; the change is left in the working tree.
