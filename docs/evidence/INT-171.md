## The k value was not invented

The threshold is stated by the blueprint in four places that agree, so nothing here chose a
number:

| source | words |
|---|---|
| files/06 §8 | "de-identified service-improvement aggregates (opt-out, k ≥ 10)" |
| files/09 §4 | "Signals (aggregated, de-identified, **k ≥ 10** per pattern)" |
| files/09 §6 | "`k_anonymity`: aggregate tables refuse patterns with n < 10" |
| files/10 §9 (learning row) | "proposal-only, **k ≥ 10**, scope tests…" |

`K_ANONYMITY_FLOOR = 10` is that value, cited at the constant. The gate still takes `k` as a
**required keyword argument with no default** — a caller states the claim it is making — and
refuses any `k` below the floor with `ThresholdBelowFloor`. A caller may raise the bar and
cannot lower it. The floor now exists in exactly one place: `intake_metrics.rollups.MIN_COHORT`
(INT-240) became an alias, because two copies of a privacy threshold are two chances for one of
them to drift down.

Nothing else was invented. No retention period, no sampling rate, no cadence and no cell-count
target appears anywhere in this change.

## What this change refuses to call de-identified

**Free text.** Not one view carries narrative, and the reason is recorded rather than assumed.
files/10 §3 states that spaCy ships no Turkish model, that realistic recall on conversational
Turkish is materially below one because the language is agglutinative and names appear in
oblique cases (*Ahmet'in*, *Ayşe'ye*), and that the old leak-rate target was unfalsifiable
because it was measured on a corpus we author. files/HUMAN_BLOCKERS.md **HB-L6** records the
same finding from the other direction: the "de-identified" claim that would justify staff read
paths over patient content *"is one Turkish NLP cannot support"*.

So the answer is **exclusion, not redaction**. `notes.payload`, `slots.value`, `documents`,
`csat_texts` and every transcript are absent from all three view definitions, and
`svc_learning_reader` holds no grant that could reach them. `tests/learning/test_analytics_views.py`
holds that line statically (the migration's DDL may not name a narrative column or read a
narrative table) and the integration lane holds it at the grant (`SELECT * FROM notes` is
`InsufficientPrivilege` for that role).

**Pseudonymised row exports.** A pseudonym is personal data; a count of pseudonyms at or above
the floor is not. Every view aggregates before it projects, so no `patient_id`, `session_id` or
`tenant_id` survives the `GROUP BY` — asserted twice, in the registry test and against
`information_schema.columns` in the integration lane.

**A clinic label.** files/09 §4 wants "≥ 2 clinics" as a stability criterion, which is a count.
`tenants` is therefore a measure and never a dimension; a small clinic is not identifiable from
these views.

## The opt-out is honoured, not merely offered

files/06 §2 makes `learning:service_improvement` an opt-out purpose and files/09 §5 promises the
opt-out "costs no feature". An opt-out that nothing enforces is the same decoration as a k helper
nobody calls, and invariant 4 requires every read of clinical data to check a consent scope — so
all three views read `analytics.v_loop2_sessions`, which drops any patient whose latest recorded
decision on that scope is a withdrawal. A later grant wins; where nothing is recorded the patient
is in, which is what `default_granted` means in the registry.

`test_the_loop_two_opt_out_is_honoured_before_the_floor_is_applied` seeds a pack of k+2 patients
of whom 3 withdrew, then asserts the two numbers separately: `sessions` holds 12 distinct
patients and the view holds 9, which is under the floor — so the view refuses a cohort the table
could have described. The base view carries row-level ids so the counting views can group over it
and is therefore granted to nobody: `test_the_learning_reader_cannot_select_the_unaggregated_base_view`
proves `has_table_privilege` is false and a direct `SELECT` is `InsufficientPrivilege`.

## Design decisions worth stating

**Subjects, not rows.** The floor is applied to `COUNT(DISTINCT patient_id)`. A chronic patient
with forty follow-ups is one person wearing forty rows, and a gate that counted rows would serve
that cell. `test_forty_sessions_from_one_patient_is_still_a_cohort_of_one` is the unit form and
`test_a_forty_session_cohort_of_one_refuses_out_of_the_database` seeds exactly that shape in
Postgres.

**Refuse the artefact, do not suppress the cell.** Suppress-and-continue hands a reader every
cell but one; the missing one comes back by subtraction from any wider aggregate over the same
population. `materialise` therefore refuses the whole view. This is also why no `HAVING` clause
was baked into the view bodies — a `HAVING` is suppression, and it would move the decision out of
the query layer where the acceptance puts it.

**The refusal is not an oracle.** It carries the view name, the threshold and a reason class. It
does not say which cell failed or how many subjects it held, and an **empty result refuses with
the identical message** as a single-subject result: distinguishing "nobody was there" from
"someone was there, but fewer than k" discloses the existence of a patient in a cell just
withheld. The refusal also never echoes a value it refused, which matters most for the free-text
case — `test_a_free_text_dimension_value_is_refused_and_never_echoed` asserts the Turkish
sentence and the name inside it appear nowhere in the exception.

**No free text, structurally.** Dimension values must be id-shaped (ASCII identifier, ≤ 64 chars)
and measures must be integers; anything else stops the view with
`dimension_not_id_shaped` / `measure_not_a_count`. "No free-text outside the review UI" is then a
property of the type rather than of a reviewer's attention, and a future view that accidentally
projects a narrative column fails on its first row instead of shipping.

**Two boundaries, neither substituting for the other.** The grant on `analytics.*` is the
narrative boundary: what `svc_learning_reader` can reach at all. The k gate at the query layer is
the small-cell boundary: what a caller may hold. The views are owner-owned rather than
`security_invoker` because a Loop-2 pattern must hold across at least two clinics and is
cross-tenant by construction (files/09 §4); the k floor plus the tenants-as-a-count rule is what
makes that bounded.

**Invariant 20.** `Cohort` is a required argument of every read and every gate call, and it
refuses an empty description or a window without timezones or without forward direction. There is
no constructor for a figure detached from the population and window that produced it;
`Measurement.provenance` renders the sentence.

**Invariant 6.** The gate logs nothing. It meters two counters whose labels are the view name and
a reason class — a refusal labelled with the offending branch or week would publish to Prometheus
exactly the cell the refusal withheld, and Prometheus retains it.

**Invariant 13.** `analytics_view_refused_total{view,reason}` and
`analytics_view_materialised_total{view}` are catalogued in `observability/metrics_catalog.yaml`
as `live`. files/08 §4 group L lists "k-anonymity rejections" as a prose KPI with no id, so the
names are ours; that list's entry was moved into the catalogue rather than left dangling.

No new service, so invariant 15 is untouched: this is a library in
`packages/intake_platform/analytics`, read by `services/learning` when INT-172 builds it.

## Deviation: the integration lane ran on its own database

The compose project (`intake-dev`) is shared by every worktree on this machine, and the shared
`intake` database was stamped at `0005_int141` — a revision id that exists on another branch and
not on `main`, so `alembic upgrade head` could not resolve it. Rather than re-stamp or drop a
database other agents are using, the integration lane ran against a database created for it
(`POSTGRES_DB=intake_int171`); every integration test and the OpenBao setup script already read
that variable. Nothing in this change depends on the database name.
