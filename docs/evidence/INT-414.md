# INT-414: Conversational question delivery — warm opener, context- and affect-aware lead-ins, questions verbatim

**Epic:** E08-mobile · **Phase:** P1 · **Sec-Row:** `mobile` · **GitHub:** #705 ·
**Depends on:** INT-415 (stacked on `int-415-delivery-profiles`) · **Branch:**
`int-414-conversational-delivery` · **Date:** 2026-09-27

## The ask

The owner's words, 27 Sep 2026: *"Make the text asked as question more natural, empathetic,
professional and welcoming at first, and the rest professional and not robot-like, and feel
dynamic with the emotions of the patient or the question context, but medical and professional."*

Everything that answers this lives in a **delivery layer around the question**: a welcome,
lead-ins, topic transitions, acknowledgements and a closing. The question itself is never
touched. CLAUDE.md's clinical rule is *"conversational delivery may paraphrase the lead-in, never
the item"*, and invariant 10 speaks scripted questions, instrument items and red-flag scripts
verbatim.

## What changed

- **Copy deck.** There are 47 new `tr` strings, each with an `en` mirror, under
  `dialogue.delivery.*`. INT-293's `dialogue.notUnderstood` is reused as the first repair
  variant rather than writing a second apology for the same event. Every slot has at least two
  variants. The full table for clinician review is below.
- **The composer** is `apps/mobile/src/lib/dialogue/delivery.ts`.
  - It is pure and synchronous, and typed over phase (opening / first / mid / sensitive /
    closing), question context (routine / sensitive / instrument / pain / red), cue, turn
    index, session seed and previous key.
  - It returns the ordered utterances `lead_in | transition | acknowledgement | question`.
    Each utterance carries a `deliveryContext` from `deliveryContext.ts`, which is
    byte-identical to INT-415's.
  - The `question` utterance is last, and its text is the text it was handed.
  - A RED script, or anything marked RED-adjacent, comes back **alone**. A step the pack
    declares `redAdjacent: true` is the `red` context (it outranks every other declaration),
    and the turn after it carries no acknowledgement and no cadence line.
  - An instrument item is checked before every other rule, so nothing is said between
    items, a re-asked item included.
  - Variation is an FNV-1a hash of the session seed and turn index, never `Math.random`.
  - Rhythm rules:
    - a turn carries at most one delivery line (the opening carries two);
    - the previous turn's line is never repeated;
    - a cadence line only follows a quiet turn, and only on about half of those;
    - a validated instrument is introduced once, and nothing is said between its items.
- **Where the words come from.** `deliveryDeck.ts` holds slot → keys and slot → delivery
  context, and has no strings. Every word is in the catalogue (invariant 5).
- **Planning.** `deliveryPlan.ts` folds the composer over a sitting.
  - It classifies a question only from what the pack **declares**:
    - `instrument` is set: instrument;
    - a 02 §33 deferral unit or a files/13 §13 `text_first` step: sensitive;
    - gated on the patient having named pain: pain;
    - anything else: routine.
  - It reads the topic from the question's 04 §5 note section, so a section change is
    announced ("Şimdi alerjilerle ilgili sorulara geçelim.").
- **Cues** are read from signals already on the device, never from audio (invariants 3 and 11):
  - a barge-in;
  - a confirmed "don't know / don't remember / prefer not to say" option;
  - a correction (the patient changed the suggested values, or chose "Düzeltmeyi söyleyin");
  - a turn that captured no words;
  - INT-293's unclear re-ask.

  `readCue` turns a missing, throwing, pending (promise) or malformed source into `"none"` on
  the spot, and the composer never awaits. The client exposes no ObserverState today, so no
  observer lane is read. The closed cue vocabulary is also why no patient word can ride in on a
  cue.
- **Typed lane** (`intake.tsx`). The line renders above the question heading, which renders
  unconditionally. A fresh intake opens with the welcome and the typed orientation; a resumed
  intake, or the steps after a voice hand-over, get "Kaldığımız yerden devam edelim."; editing
  one answer from the review gets nothing. The line is pure and synchronous, so the question is
  never late for it.
- **Voice lane** (`useDialogueSession.ts`, `DialogueIntake.tsx`).
  - A question turn is spoken as its planned utterances, in order, through the existing TTS
    lane. It uses one player at a time, and a barge-in stops the chain.
  - Each delivery line is its own quiet bubble ahead of the verbatim question bubble, which is
    the INT-293 pattern. The reducer's `agent_unclear` entry no longer renders separately,
    because its sentence now travels, spoken as well as shown, in the re-asked question's plan.
  - `settleTurnClips` drops any lead-in whose clip is not made yet, so a lead-in costs the
    seconds it takes to *say* and never the seconds it takes to *make*. Only the opening waits
    for its own welcome, bounded by the clip deadline.
  - The transcript shows what the voice said. `settleTurnClips` reports which lead-ins it
    kept (`spoken`, one flag per delivery line), and `shownDelivery` narrows the plan to
    those lines. A lead-in dropped for want of a clip is dropped from the screen too, and a
    turn whose clips are still settling claims no line yet. A turn with no speech attempted
    (typed, muted, failed) shows its plan as written.
  - Pre-warm makes the welcome with the first question, and the rest of the spoken deck only
    after every question.
- **Delivery context reaches the voice lane (stacked on INT-415).**
  - `ClipStore.ensure(name, text, context)` hands the context to the synthesizer.
    `georgePreferringSynth` forwards it to `fetchGeorgeClip(..., { context })`; the self-hosted
    lane ignores it.
  - Questions and their read-backs use the question's own context. Delivery lines use their
    slot's context, except repair and silence, which follow the question.
  - The context is part of a delivery clip's name, so a cache hit is never the wrong profile.
  - Nothing in the dialogue is ever sent as `red`.
- **Only catalogue text reaches a synthesizer.**
  - `scriptedDeliveryTr(key)` mints a `ScriptedTr` from a deck key and nothing else.
    `requireScriptedDeliveryTr` refuses anything that is not exactly that key's line. Patient
    words stay unmintable.
  - The George route stays catalogue-only (INT-326). `intake_api/dev_voice_delivery.py` holds
    the 42 lines the voice lane can speak, verbatim from `tr` and pinned to it by test from both
    sides. `with_delivery_lines` widens a catalogue **that loaded**, through
    `dataclasses.replace`, so INT-415's red and instrument pins carry over.
  - An empty or unreadable manifest set still leaves the lane unconfigured.
  - A delivery line carries no pin, so the route refuses it under `red` (tested).
- **Closing** is on the review screen, shown only for a complete intake that is not
  checkpointed and not yet sent. It is display only, because the dialogue's audio is torn down
  when that screen opens (INT-233 audio hygiene).

## The opening

On the voice lane, a fresh intake opens with two utterances and then the first question
verbatim. The welcome is one of three variants, stable for a visit:

> **Hoş geldiniz. Muayenenizden önce size bazı sorular soracağım.**
> **Soruları ekranda da görebilirsiniz. Dilerseniz yazarak da yanıt verebilirsiniz.**

The other welcome variants are *"Merhaba, hoş geldiniz. Muayeneden önce birlikte bazı sorular
üzerinden geçelim."* and *"Hoş geldiniz. Muayenenize hazırlık için size bazı sorular sormak
istiyorum."*. The second voice orientation is *"Ben konuşurken araya girebilirsiniz. Sorular
ekranda yazılı olarak da durur."*

Two things the brief allowed were **deliberately left out**:

1. **"Your answers go to your doctor."** `docs/legal/strings.yaml` does not exist (INT-210;
   `docs/legal/COUNSEL_BRIEF.md` records the absence). Consent and legal wording renders only
   from there (invariant 18), so no delivery line says where answers go. A test forbids
   `hekim|doktor|paylaş|iletil|gönderil` in the deck.
2. **"Say 'tekrar' to hear it again."** The dev dialogue has no spoken repeat command. A
   "tekrar" utterance is read as an answer that maps to nothing and is re-asked later, so the
   line would promise behaviour the product does not have. A test forbids "tekrar" in the deck.
   The opening instead states the affordances that are real: the question is on screen, typing
   works at any time, and barge-in works.
