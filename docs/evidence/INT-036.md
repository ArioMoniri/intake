## How the acceptance clauses are pinned

- **6-month floor (DEST-2017)**: `periodic_destruction.interval_months: 6`
  (schema caps it at ≤ 6 — a longer interval would violate RG 30224).
  `DestructionEngine` finds its own last `retention.periodic_run` entry on
  the audit chain and raises `PeriodicRunTooSoonError` (naming the next
  allowed instant) for anything younger; `run_dsar` is the separate targeted
  path the floor never blocks, and it is logged the same way. Tests:
  `test_the_six_month_floor_blocks_an_early_run`,
  `test_dsar_destruction_is_not_blocked_by_the_floor_and_is_logged`.
- **Destruction log kept 3 years**: the log IS the hash chain —
  `retention.destroyed` / `retention.dsar_destroyed` entries carry
  `class:<data_class>:count:<n>` (counts + classes, ids only, invariant 6)
  plus one run-summary entry per run. The WORM audit copy's 10-year
  retention covers DEST-2017's ≥ 3-year duty; the policy declares
  `destruction_log.min_retention_years: 3` (schema floor ge=3) and lists it
  under `protected`. Type-level indestructibility: `DestroyableClass` cannot
  name `audit_chain`/`audit_records`/`destruction_log`, the loader refuses a
  destroyable row with those names (`ProtectedClassError`), and
  `DocumentObjectDestroyer` refuses the WORM audit bucket
  (`WormBundleProtectedError`). Tests:
  `test_destruction_log_kept_three_years_on_the_audit_chain`,
  `test_loader_refuses_protected_classes`,
  `test_enum_cannot_name_the_audit_chain_or_the_log`,
  `test_destruction_log_rows_survive_class_purges`,
  `test_document_object_destroyer_refuses_the_worm_audit_bucket`.
- **Raw-audio guard extended**: the strongest retention statement about audio
  is that there is nothing to retain. The policy must carry
  `raw_audio: never_written` (`RawAudioRowMissingError` otherwise), no
  `classes` entry may match the audio pattern
  (`AudioRetentionClassError`; parametrised over raw_audio /
  session_audio_cache / pcm_windows / call_recording), and the enum has no
  audio member. This is the policy half of `audio_no_write_guard`
  (files/10 §9.1 inv. 27).
- **Lane windows zeroing verified**: verified at the policy layer — the
  memory_only row must name its triggers (schema) and the test pins the set
  to exactly the six of 06 §3. The runtime property itself (buffers all-zero
  after every trigger, inv. 18 `audio_zeroization_guard`) belongs to the
  voice-runtime issues — `services/voice-runtime` does not exist yet, so this
  issue delivers the machine-readable contract that guard will assert
  against, not a claim about code that is not there.
- **Time travel**: every schedule test hands instants around the calendar
  boundary (`add_months`, month-end clamped) into the pure planner —
  1 y − 1 s not due / 1 y due, same at 10 y, token expiry, account deletion;
  `clinical_record` and both draft classes stay refused even 100 years out.

## For the human (stop-and-ask territory, per CLAUDE.md)

Two 06 §3 rows have no sourced duration, so the engine **refuses to destroy
them** until a human supplies the figure (never invented here):

1. `consent_records_consumer_tenant` — 06 §3 scopes "10 years" to
   "(hospital tenants)"; the INTAKE-as-controller consumer deployment needs
   its own retention decision (owner + counsel; likely the same 10 years,
   but that is exactly the sentence a human must say).
2. `research_extracts` — "per ethics protocol": the registered study's
   protocol (site question: HUMAN_BLOCKERS.md HB-L1) supplies the duration
   when it exists.

Related but not this issue's to fix: files/06 line 80 already flags that the
KSVY 2025 amendment raised deceased-person retention to 30 years and that
§3/10 §2 were written against the 20-year text **[counsel]** — 06 §3 has no
deceased-person row today, so no policy class exists for it either.
