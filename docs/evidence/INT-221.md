## The one decision that shapes it

The registry assertion is the *provable* half and it is fully buildable, so it
is built to fail closed twice: `attest_residency` reads the registry as **raw
yaml** and asserts every `phi_path: true` row is `self_hostable: true`
(independent of the pydantic model, so it bites even if the loader's own
validator were relaxed), **and** re-loads through the typed `load_registry`
(schema + the same invariant); then it runs
`intake_platform.egress.assert_dev_only_vendors` over the committed
allow-list. Two committed sources, cross-checked, a violation raising rather
than degrading.

The **Genelge 2019/12 attestation** is the human half. A formal attestation to
the Ministry that health data is in-country and vendor-free is a *legal
statement* — CLAUDE.md makes consent/legal text a stop-and-ask and
HUMAN_BLOCKERS Rule 1 forbids authoring a legal value. So the mechanism ships
with the statement and signatory `None` and `render()` refusing until a human
signs it, exactly as INT-210's `LegalDocumentUnsigned` and INT-298's
`pack_signoff` do. The blocker is **HB-L11** (whether the residency posture is
legally attestable under KVKK Art. 9); the signature regime is HB-L27. No new
HB or ADR id was minted.

The module lives in `intake_probes` because it must read **both** the registry
(owned by `intake_probes.registry`) and the egress allow-list (owned by
`intake_platform.egress`); `intake_probes` already depends on `intake_platform`,
so the edge is one-way and acyclic.
