# INT-583: new-device sign-in notice by push to the previously signed-in device(s), never SMS (D35)

**Date:** 30 October 2026 · **Branch:** `int-583-push-new-device-notice` (stacked on
`int-580-sms-hijacking`) · **Issue:** #982 · **Sec-Row:** files/10 §9 `onboarding`

Owner decision D35 (28 Oct 2026, files/00_STATE.md §3): the new-device sign-in notice goes by
push to the patient's previously signed-in device(s), never by SMS. A SIM swap would deliver an
SMS notice to the attacker, who is exactly who signed in. INT-580 detects the new device (the
`sec_new_device_logins` counter, the `auth.login.new_device` audit row) and hands it to a notice
port that failed closed. This issue builds the push path behind that port.

## The route: direct APNs, not a relay

The notice goes to iOS devices only. Android cannot sign in on a real mode until Play
Integrity proves a key (HB-A3), so there is never an Android device to tell. Two routes reach
an iPhone:

- **Direct APNs (chosen).** Apple's push service is the one processor that no iOS push can
  avoid. The `.p8` signing key stays in OpenBao. The processor list grows by Apple alone, and the
  egress list by Apple's two hosts. The cost is HTTP/2, which APNs requires: `h2` (with `hpack`
  and `hyperframe`) joins the API's dependencies.
- **A relay such as Expo's push service (rejected).** It would be a second processor abroad
  holding every device token and every notice. For Expo it would also hold our APNs signing key,
  uploaded to its credential store. It would add a third egress host (`exp.host`) and an Expo
  access token in OpenBao. All it saves is HTTP/2.

**Licences of the new dependency**, each verified from the raw LICENSE blob on 30 Oct 2026:
`h2` 4.4.1 (`python-hyper/h2`), `hpack` 4.2.0 and `hyperframe` 6.1.0. All three are MIT. On the
app side, `expo-notifications` 57.0.24 is MIT (the expo/expo monorepo).

**Apple's wire contract** was read from Apple's documentation on 30 Oct 2026, through its
documentation JSON endpoints (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/usernotifications/<page>.json`):

- "Sending notification requests to APNs" gives:
  - the hosts `api.push.apple.com:443` and `api.sandbox.push.apple.com:443`, over HTTP/2;
  - `POST /3/device/<token>`;
  - the headers `authorization: bearer`, `apns-push-type`, `apns-topic`, `apns-priority`,
    `apns-expiration` and `apns-collapse-id`, the last at most 64 bytes;
  - a payload of at most 4096 bytes.
- "Establishing a token-based connection to APNs" gives the ES256 provider token:
  - header `{alg, kid}`, claims `{iss: team id, iat}`;
  - renewed no more often than every 20 minutes and no less often than every 60.
- "Handling notification responses from APNs" gives every status, every reason string, and
  the `{reason, timestamp}` body.

`tests/fixtures/apns/responses.json` is derived from these pages. It was never captured from a
live call, and it must be re-recorded against the sandbox once the owner's key is stored.

## What was built

**API (`services/api/src/intake_api/`):**

- **`push_port.py`.** The gateway port: a `PushAlert` built from constants, a closed
  `PushFailure` vocabulary, a `PushUnconfigured` reason, and a `PushGateway` that never
  retries.
- **`push_apns.py`.** The one adapter. It runs over the INT-018 guarded async transport with an
  HTTP/2 inner transport, declaring `EgressPurpose.PUSH_SECURITY_NOTICE` (new) and
  `DataClass.PHI`.
  - Credentials come from OpenBao `secret/api/apns` only (`key_p8`, `key_id`, `team_id`).
    Their shapes are checked and a P-256 key is required. A refusal names the field, never the
    value, and the key never reaches `repr`.
  - The provider token is renewed every 50 minutes, and again after a 403
    `ExpiredProviderToken` or `InvalidProviderToken`.
  - 410 (`Unregistered`, `ExpiredToken`), 400 `BadDeviceToken` and 400
    `DeviceTokenNotForTopic` are `token_gone`, which drops the registration. Every other
    documented answer maps to one closed reason. The APNs reason string is logged only when it
    is one of Apple's documented strings.
  - httpx's own INFO request line carries the full URL, and therefore the token. A filter on
    the `httpx` logger redacts it before any handler sees the line. The tests found this one;
    without the filter the token reached the log.
  - The APNs host follows App Attest's environment, because one provisioning profile carries
    both entitlements: sandbox only for exactly `development`.
  - Wiring checks egress before the vault, so a refused host never has its key read. It
    returns the gateway, or a typed `PushUnconfigured`.
- **`sign_in_notice.py` and `sign_in_notice.tr.json`.**
  - The INT-580 port moves here. It is now told which device to leave out
    (`new_device_sign_in(patient_id, new_device_ref)`) and answers with one `NoticeOutcome`
    per device tried.
  - `PushSignInNotice` sends to every signed-in device with a token, except the new device.
    It is rate-limited, and it drops a gone token.
  - `NoSignInNoticeChannel(reason)` is what is wired with no channel, and it logs its reason.
  - The copy is one tr resource: "Hesabınıza yeni bir cihazdan giriş yapıldı. Bu giriş sizin
    değilse uygulamadaki Cihazlarınız ekranından o cihazın oturumunu kapatabilirsiniz." The
    title is "Önvizit".
- **`device_sessions.py`.** The Redis store:
  - `auth:sess:<patient>`: per device hash, a random session id, its refresh families and its
    last sign-in;
  - `auth:push:<patient>`: the APNs token, sealed with AES-256-GCM under
    `OtpPepper.push_token_key()`, with `patient|device` as associated data;
  - `auth:push:rate:<patient>`: three notices per rolling hour.
- **`session_routes.py`.** These routes sit under `/v1/auth` with the pre-auth marker, and only
  the OTP tier's DPoP session reaches them:
  - `GET /v1/auth/sessions`;
  - `DELETE /v1/auth/sessions/{session_id}`;
  - `POST /v1/auth/sessions/sign-out-others` (`{"not_me": bool}`);
  - `PUT /v1/auth/push-token` (`{"platform": "ios", "token": <hex>}`);
  - `DELETE /v1/auth/push-token`.

  The 24-hour hold: a session younger than `SIGN_OUT_OTHERS_HOLD_S` may sign itself out but not
  the others. `not_me` is the breach hook: it writes `auth.session.disowned`, counts
  `sec_sign_in_disowned_total` and pages `SignInDisowned`, even under the hold.
- **`otp.py`.**
  - The verify binds the session to its device (`DeviceSessionStore.bind`) under a family id it
    mints.
  - The notice is told after the response, like an SMS send (INT-579), and each outcome is
    audited as `auth.notice.<outcome>` under the patient.
  - `resolve_access` refuses an access token whose family is gone (`AuthStore.family_alive`),
    so a signed-out device loses its access tokens at once.
- **`app.py`.** `_sign_in_notice_from_settings` wires the push channel or no channel, and sets
  the `push_channel_unconfigured{reason}` gauge.

**Mobile (`apps/mobile`).** These were built by a mobile subagent against this contract and
reviewed here:

- `expo-notifications` behind one lazy `await import("expo-notifications")` in
  `src/lib/push/native.ts`. It reads the native APNs device token and never an Expo token.
  `e2e/notifications.spec.ts` is narrowed so that this one file may name the package and
  nothing anywhere schedules or presents a notification.
- A pure planner (`src/lib/push/plan.ts`):
  - registration is silent only when permission is already granted;
  - the system prompt appears only from the "Bildirimleri açın" button;
  - a denial is explained ("Telefonunuzun Ayarlar bölümünden açabilirsiniz"), never thrown;
  - kiosk mode never registers.
- The "Cihazlarınız" screen (`src/app/(tabs)/me/sessions.tsx`), linked from Profilim and
  registered in the tabs shell. It lists devices with this device first, signs one or all the
  others out, offers "Bu girişi ben yapmadım" with a confirmation, and explains the hold.
- Sign-out drops this device's push registration (best-effort and lazy).
- The tr keys (`sessions.*`) are in siz, B1, and pass the i18n register gates.
- One correction was made here. The subagent read refusals as FastAPI's nested
  `{"detail": {...}}`, but the API flattens every refusal to `{"error": ...}`
  (`app.py` `_machine_readable_errors`). The client now reads the top level. A near-miss test
  shows the nested shape is not read.

**Owner tooling.** `infra/staging/apns_secrets.sh` syncs `/intake/staging/apns_{key_p8,key_id,team_id}`
from SSM into OpenBao `secret/api/apns`. It writes on stdin, prints field names only, and
prints `changed` or `unchanged`. The API's OpenBao policy gains read on `secret/data/api/apns`.

## The inventory annotation (proposed for the legal lane)

There are two new off-schema flows (`intake_platform.store.facts`); the store labels are
generated from them.

| flow | category | purpose | basis | retention | recipients |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `device_sessions` | `pseudonymised_identity` | show the patient the devices their account is signed in on, let them sign one or all others out, or report a sign-in that was not theirs | `BASIS_LEGAL_OBLIGATION` (KVKK m.5/2-ç with m.12) | 30 days after the last sign-in (`REFRESH_TTL_S`), or at once on sign-out | `REC_INTERNAL` |
| `push_tokens` | `pseudonymised_identity` | tell the patient, on the devices already signed in, that their account was signed in from a new device (D35); never used for any other message | `BASIS_LEGAL_OBLIGATION` (KVKK m.5/2-ç with m.12) | until the device is signed out, the app unregisters it, Apple reports it gone, or 30 days after it was last registered | `REC_PUSH_PROCESSOR`: Apple Push Notification service, as processor, receives the token with each notice once the owner's APNs egress row is applied |

`REC_PUSH_PROCESSOR` is classified as `Sharing.NONE`: a processor delivering our own notice to
the patient's own device, on our instruction, is not a disclosure to a third party. The basis
mirrors `sign_in_devices`, which the legal review of 30 Oct 2026 confirmed on condition. Both
are engineering's proposal. `docs/legal/synthetic/documents.yaml` (`aydinlatma.app`) records the
question for counsel, including whether the notice and the core text must name Apple's push
service.

## Residuals

- A SIM-swapped intruder can sign in again by SMS after being signed out. Only the passkey
  step-up (INT-582, D36) or an operator lock ends that, and the runbook says to lock.
- A patient whose only device is the lost one gets no notice. There is nobody else to tell.
- On the synthetic host every network is internal. That gap is closed by ADR-045 (below):
  the push rides its own proxy, `push-egress`. Its config admits nothing until the owner's
  rows.
- A development-signed build registers a sandbox token. Against the production host APNs
  answers `BadDeviceToken`, and the token is dropped.
- The 24-hour hold also stops a patient who signs in on a new phone from signing a lost phone
  out at once. The runbook's operator path covers that.
- Two engineering defaults: `SIGN_OUT_OTHERS_HOLD_S` = 24 h and `NOTICE_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` = 3
  (below which the phone's 3-codes-an-hour budget already sits). `NOTICE_EXPIRY_S` = 24 h is how
  long APNs keeps an undelivered notice.

## ADR-045: the push route on the synthetic host

After the first hand-back, the orchestrator asked for the infra gap to be closed inside
INT-583, so that the owner's three steps are enough. It allocated ADR-045
(`docs/adr/ADR-045-the-push-notice-rides-its-own-egress-proxy.md`), which amends ADR-042.

- **`intake_platform.egress`.** `DEDICATED_PERSONAL_PROXIES` gains
  `PUSH_SECURITY_NOTICE → http://push-egress:3128`, an exact match. `PURPOSE_PROXIES` follows,
  so the push rides only its own proxy, and that proxy carries no other purpose.
- **`scripts/security/render_push_egress_conf.py`** renders
  `infra/staging/push-egress/squid.conf` from the allow-list's `push_security_notice` rows.
  It follows the SMS renderer line for line: CONNECT only, exact hosts on 443, the rebinding
  window closed, the denied ranges refused, and a `host:port` log. With no row (the shipped
  state) it admits nothing.
- **`infra/staging/compose.yaml`.**
  - It adds the `push-egress` service: the same digest-pinned squid, run as uid 13, read-only,
    with no capabilities, no published port, `core: 0` and a listener healthcheck.
  - It adds the networks `push-out` (internal, shared with the API alone) and `push-wan` (the
    proxy's way off the host).
  - The API joins `push-out`, depends on `push-egress` being started, and names it in
    `INTAKE_PUSH_EGRESS_PROXY_URL`.
- **The monitoring contract (invariants 13 and 15).** `observability/registry.yaml` carries
  `push-egress`: self-hostable, a `tcp_connect` probe, the squid licence record, and
  `self_hosted_now: false` until a deploy runs it. `infra/staging/inventory.yaml` places it as
  `needed: false` (`fails_closed_without_it`) and `unprobed: outside_probe_networks`.
- **Recorded fixtures.** The probe sweeps (`tests/fixtures/reliability/probe_sweep.json`,
  `tests/fixtures/ops/host_sweep.json`) and the admin fixtures they feed (models, ops,
  reliability) were re-taken with the shipped recorders, with nothing hand-edited.
  - The recordings were made without the local deid cache, so that cache's row keeps its
    previously recorded state.
  - Against the old sweep, two things changed: `push-egress` was added, and postgres's
    refusal text and latency moved. `overview.spec.ts` now pins the re-taken latency of 626 ms.
  - The other fixtures `make admin-fixtures` rewrote (date and sha drift only) were left
    unchanged.
- **The adapter and its wiring.**
  - `ApnsPushGateway` takes `proxy=` and refuses any proxy but the dedicated one at
    construction. Through the proxy the hostname survives to the CONNECT, and the allow-list,
    purpose and gate still decide first.
  - HTTP/2 runs inside the CONNECT tunnel (httpcore's tunnel connection negotiates `h2` by
    ALPN).
  - The wiring moved to `intake_api.push_wiring`, which keeps `push_apns.py` under 500 lines.
    It refuses a wrong proxy, and on the synthetic host a missing one, with the new typed
    reason `no_route`, before any key is read.
- **The vendor tripwire, re-cut, for the legal lane to verify.**
  `tests/legal/test_synthetic_vendor_tripwire.py` gains the service and the two networks in
  `VERIFIED`, a pin that the push proxy carries the push purpose alone, and three near misses:
  the push and SMS proxies bridged, probes on `push-out`, and the API on `push-wan`. The push
  carries a device token and constant words, never audio, answers or documents. No text names
  Apple's push service yet; that is the open `aydinlatma.app` question.
- **The owner patch** (`owner-583-apns-egress.patch`) now also re-renders the push proxy's
  config and flips its shipped-state pin to "admits Apple's two hosts alone".
