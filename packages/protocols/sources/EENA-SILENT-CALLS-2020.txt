EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Dealing with silent, hang-up, and
abandoned 112 calls can be a real
challenge for emergency services. In
many cases, such calls may be genuine
emergency calls made by people with
hearing or speaking impairments, by
people who lost consciousness after they
initiated the call, or by people who
cannot speak because they are involved
in dangerous situations like robberies,
kidnappings etc.
Furthermore, hang-up calls may occur in
cases of network unavailability or in case of
failure or damage of the device initiating. In
most of the above situations, calling back is
not an option for the emergency services,
because the caller may not be able or willing
to respond or because calling back is
impossible.

Silent Emergency Calls
Type of call
Hearing/speaking-impaired person
Situation does not allow caller to speak

Person unconscious or unable to speak

Comments / examples
The person can be in a real emergency situation
but is not able to speak and/or hear that the call
has been answered.
The caller is in an emergency situation and is
able to establish a 112 call, but the situation does
not allow the person to speak (e.g. kidnapping).
The caller has been able to dial 112 but after
establishing the call, the person is no longer able
to communicate (e.g. the caller loses
consciousness).
