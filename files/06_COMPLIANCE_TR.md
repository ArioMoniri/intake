> This file is written to be **relied on**, not hedged: each obligation cites the instrument (Resmî Gazete date/no where verified in this research pass), states what INTAKE must do, and names the backlog issue(s) that implement it (codes resolve in `11_ISSUE_BACKLOG.md`). ## 0. Firm legal positioning of the product
1. **Two data-protection roles, by deployment.** (a) *Consumer app (patient-owned record)*: INTAKE (the company) is **veri sorumlusu / controller** (KVKK m.3). (b) *Hospital deployment (clinic console, kiosk, connectors)*: the hospital is controller; INTAKE is **veri işleyen / processor** under a written DPA — the same role the SBYS regulation assigns to hospital-software vendors. Every table, screen and export knows which role applies (tenant flag; INT-030, INT-250).
2. **Not a remote-health service.** INTAKE collects and structures patient-reported information *before* a visit and delivers it to the clinician; no health professional provides examination, consultation or treatment *through* INTAKE. Therefore the Uzaktan Sağlık Hizmetlerinin Sunumu Hakkında Yönetmelik (RG 31746, 10.02.2022) does not govern the intake itself. The moment any tenant wants clinician↔patient interaction through us (the `teleconsult` visit type merely *prepares* for the hospital's own tele-visit), that feature requires the facility's MoH uzaktan-sağlık permit and a **registered USBS** (Yönetmelik m.5, m.14(a)); we do not build it before USBS tescili — enforced as a feature gate, not a promise.
3. **Medical-device boundary is explicit and flag-enforced.** Under the Tıbbi Cihaz Yönetmeliği (RG 31499 mükerrer, 02.06.2021 — full MDR 2017/745 alignment) and **MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (17 June 2025)** — *the English Rev.1 that TİTCK itself hosts, **not** TİTCK's own Turkish translation, which renders the 2019 original; corrected 25 Aug 2026 because Rev.1 changed text this file relies on (see the NLP note at the end of this item)* — two functions are **MDSW**: automated red-flag triage that directs a patient's urgency, and automated interpretation of screening instruments. Classification: Annex VIII **Rule 11 → Class IIb for both device functions**, with a Class III argument that must be rebutted in writing — rationale documented, notified body to confirm (INT-216) **[counsel]**. *(Corrected 25 Aug 2026. The previous "Class IIa" position is not defensible for three reasons. (1) IMDRF N12 — the framework MDCG 2019-11 Annex III explicitly adopts — defines "drive clinical management" as including "**triage** early signs of a disease or condition". Critical situation x drive = IMDRF III.i = **Class IIb**. (2) MDCG says to assess the impact "**if based on incorrect information from the MDSW**". The foreseeable incorrect output is a **false negative** — a missed MI or stroke, so the patient does not call 112 and presents outside the thrombolysis/PCI window. That is the death/irreversible limb, i.e. the **Class III** argument. The former rationale assessed false positives only, which is not the test. (3) Decisively, **MDCG 2019-11 Annex IV contains our second device function almost verbatim**: "Diagnostic MDSW intended for scoring depression based on inputted data on a patient's symptoms (e.g. mood, anxiety) should be classified as **class IIb** under Rule 11(a) (IMDRF Risk Category III.ii)". Instrument interpretation is therefore not arguable. Note also MDCG's nearest analogue for the triage function: a heartbeat app that detects abnormalities and **informs a physician** is IIb — our output instructs a **lay patient** to call an ambulance with no clinician in between, which cannot be a lower class. IIa would be reachable only with an intended purpose materially narrower than §0.3 — no urgency direction, no condition-specific red flags, emergency instruction as a static non-algorithmic notice — which is precisely `device_functions=off`. **Stage A is the coherent half of the plan; the former Stage B IIa target was the incoherent half.** Consequences to re-plan on: Annex IX Ch. II technical-documentation assessment per generic device group rather than IIa sampling; clinical investigation generally expected rather than literature-based equivalence; **PSUR annually (IIb) rather than >= every 2 years (IIa)**; longer notified-body queue and higher cost. **Buy a written NB classification opinion before INT-217's scope and budget are fixed** — a EUR 5-15k question guarding a seven-figure programme, and a IIa->IIb surprise at classification review is a ~9-12 month schedule break. Implementing rule 3.5 requires the strictest applicable rule, so INT-216 must carry **one classification rationale per device function**, not one for the product.)* Everything else (structured intake, summaries, record organisation, Doctor Link transport) is non-device documentation/communication software per MDCG 2019-11.
   **Firm two-stage plan:** *Stage A (P0–P1):* commercial tenants run with `device_functions=off` — no automated patient-facing urgency advice, no automated severity labels; red-flag answers are displayed to clinicians as patient-reported data; fixed "Acil bir durumda 112'yi arayın" text appears at static points; the deterministic triage runs in **shadow** to build clinical-evaluation evidence. The Acıbadem pilot runs `device_functions=on` **only** as a registered **clinical investigation**: KAEK ethics approval + TİTCK permission under the Tıbbi Cihaz Klinik Araştırmaları Yönetmeliği and 3359 s. Kanun Ek m.10 (approval and permission are required for *all* device studies, observational included), with insurance as required and the study ID bound to the tenant flag (INT-117, INT-218). *Stage B (P2–P3):* ISO 13485 QMS, IEC 62304 **Ed 1.1 (2006+A1:2015)** lifecycle — ⚠️ **class B is ASSERTED, NOT DETERMINED, and is probably wrong**: clause 4.3(a) puts software in **class C** where it can contribute to a hazardous situation whose possible harm is death or serious injury *after external risk control measures*, and **under-triage on an emergency presentation is exactly that**. Stage A (`device_functions=off`, no automated severity label) arguably **is** the external control that reduces it; **Stage B is not**, and the clinical investigation runs `device_functions=on`. AMD1 4.3(f) permits a lower class for a documented group of items — the hook for segregating a **class C triage core** from a class A/B intake shell — but **5.3.5 segregation is itself [Class C]** and must state how effectiveness is ensured. **This is an architectural decision, not a lookup, and it gets much more expensive after the code exists (HB-L17)**, ISO 14971 risk file, IEC 62366-1 usability, IEC 81001-5-1 security lifecycle, clinical evaluation per MDCG 2020-1, notified-body conformity assessment, PRRC appointment (m.15), UDI + **ÜTS** registration under TİTCK's TCKKD-KLVZ-03 guide (in force 13.05.2026) — then `device_functions=on` commercially (INT-217/219/220).

   **Rev.1 closes the "simple search" safe harbour by name, for NLP — and our extractor is an LLM.** *(Added 25 Aug 2026. This text did not exist when `03 §10` was written.)* **MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 §3.1, verbatim:** *"software would not be considered as conducting 'Simple search' if it contributes to achieving a medical purpose. E.g. … **a software that performs search using Natural Language Processing (NLP)** where those actions contribute to achieving a medical purpose."* The document-ingestion extractor (`03 §10`) is precisely an NLP pass over clinical text, so the "simple search" route is no longer available to it by name.
   **The honest position, stated as a position and not as reassurance:** this does **not** by itself make extraction a device function. Qualification still turns on **medical purpose** — §3.1: *"Software must have a medical purpose on its own to be qualified as a MDSW"* — and §3.1 still lists **"data parsing"** among the non-medical purposes, which is what M0 does: it lifts a number off a lab report and asks the patient to confirm it, asserting nothing about the number. **But it removes the cleanest argument we had, and it makes the intended-purpose wording load-bearing in a way it was not before.** *"To parse patient-supplied documents into structured items for the patient's own confirmation"* is defensible; *"to identify abnormal results"* is not — and the distance between those two sentences is one prompt change. **[counsel]**
   **Consequence for the notified-body engagement already budgeted in this item:** the extraction module now needs **its own classification rationale**, under the §3.4 one-rationale-per-module rule (§0.6) — not merely the two device functions. Put the extraction question in the same letter as the NB classification opinion bought before INT-217's scope is fixed: it costs nothing extra there, and a great deal at classification review.
4. **No diagnosis, ever.** 1219 sayılı Kanun m.1/m.8 reserves diagnosis and treatment to licensed physicians; every note and summary carries the fixed sentence *"Bu belge hastanın beyanına dayanır; hekim değerlendirmesinin yerine geçmez."* and the app never names a suspected diagnosis to the patient (PolicyGuard rule + INT-084 golden).
5. **Learning under change control.** Because triage-adjacent content is (or will be) device content, Loop 2 proposals touching device functions go through QMS design-change control in addition to clinician review; the proposal pipeline is technically unable to modify red-flag levels, instruments or consent texts (INT-172; `09 §4`).

6. **The product is four modules, and each carries its own intended purpose.** MDCG 2019-11 **§3.4** (unchanged in Rev. 1, 17 June 2025): *"It is the obligation of the manufacturer to identify the boundaries and the interfaces of the different modules"* — with the companion obligation that **the manufacturer must demonstrate that the non-medical modules do not compromise the medical ones.** So INT-216 carries **one qualification rationale per module**, matching the "one per device function" rule already stated in §0.3. Architecture in `03 §4.7.1`.

   | module | contains | qualification | intended purpose as filed |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **M0 `intake_core`** | transcription, slot capture, **document extraction**, note assembly, transport | non-device (documentation/communication software) — ⚠ **extraction carries its own written rationale** since MDCG Rev.1 §3.1 (§0.3) | to collect, structure and transmit patient-reported information before a visit; **to parse patient-supplied documents into structured items for the patient's own confirmation** |
   | **M1 `interaction_signals`** | `affect` · `engagement` · `asr_quality` | non-device | *"to adapt the pace, register and input modality of a data-collection conversation"* |
   | **M2 `clinical_signals`** | crisis-lexicon signposting; instrument scheduling | signposting non-device; **instrument scheduling under `device_functions`** | to route deterministic safety signposting, and to schedule validated instruments |
   | **M3 `acoustic`** | DAM-class voice biomarker | **device — investigational only** (§0.7) | to compute a voice-derived depression/anxiety score within a registered clinical investigation |

   **The M1 boundary is a type, not a policy**, which is why it is auditable: the only thing that crosses it is a closed `InteractionState` record of six fields (`{pace, modality, break_offer, confirm_by_card, repeat_request, third_party_present}`). No emotion label, confidence score, numeric affect value or free text crosses. The emotion inference exists inside M1 and dies there. **The test that asserts this (`interaction_state_closed_domain`, `10 §9.1` inv. 22) is filed as evidence** for the §3.4 non-interference obligation — a test, not a paragraph of assurance.

   **The counterintuitive part, recorded because it keeps being re-derived backwards: "clinician-only" is not the safe half of the design, it is the half that creates the device function.** MDCG's own worked examples turn on informing a professional — a heartbeat-analysis app that analyses and **informs a physician** is Class IIb. *Not rendered at all* is the safe direction. Hence the deletion of the clinician-note `affect_summary` sentence in favour of the objective **"Konuşma özellikleri"** block (`03 §4.7.6`, `04 §5`): measured speech rate, pause ratio, F0 mean/SD and response latency, each with a population reference range and a recording-condition caveat. Data asserting no clinical construct, and more use to the clinician than the sentence was.

   **On FDA — Device Example 14 is not binding here, and the reason is the point.** §520(o)(1)(E) FD&C Act is a **statutory device *exclusion* unique to the United States**. MDR 2017/745 and the Tıbbi Cihaz Yönetmeliği (RG 31499 mükerrer, 02.06.2021) contain **no CDS exclusion at all** — there is no Criterion 1 to fail, because there is no carve-out to qualify for. **This is worse for us, not better.** The only question is MDCG 2019-11's decision path, where a signal-analysis architecture is neither disqualifying nor protective and **intended purpose is the whole test**. Example 14 goes into the technical file as **known adverse authority that has been addressed**, not omitted — an omitted adverse authority found by a reviewer costs more than an addressed one (`13 §6`; `00 §3` D7).

7. **`acoustic` is an investigational device module, and there is no shadow mode for it.** **MDCG 2019-11 Annex IV, verbatim:** *"Diagnostic MDSW intended for scoring depression based on inputted data on a patient's symptoms (e.g. mood, anxiety) should be classified as class IIb under Rule 11(a) (IMDRF Risk Category III.ii)."* A voice-derived depression/anxiety score mapped onto PHQ-9/GAD-7 is that *a fortiori*, and **no labelling, disclaimer or "research flag" changes qualification.**

   **Stated plainly: making `acoustic` clinician-visible in a commercial tenant before CE/TİTCK conformity assessment is not lawful.** The lawful home is the investigational-device route — MDR Art. 62 ff. / TCY m.61 ff. bind devices **placed on the market or put into service**, and a device used exclusively within a registered clinical investigation is investigational, not marketed.

   Registration under **TCKAY (RG 31890, 08.07.2022)**, and the sequence is not negotiable:
   - ⚠️ **CORRECTION (was "ATADEK" throughout this file until 2026-08-27).** Acıbadem runs **two** committees and the previous text named the wrong one. **KAEK** — *Klinik Araştırmalar Etik Kurulu* — takes studies **within** the klinik araştırmalar yönetmeliği scope, which is ours; **ATADEK** takes those **outside** it, and its own application form routes device studies away in terms (*"tıbbi cihaz … Klinik Etik Kurula başvurunuz"*). **TCKAY m.5/3 routes device investigations to TİTCK's approved-committee list, and only KAEK is on it** — `2025-KAEK-51`, Aktif. **An ATADEK opinion would not satisfy m.5/3.** Submissions go to **kaek@acibadem.edu.tr**.
   - **KAEK ethics first, then TİTCK — strictly sequential per m.8/2**: the ethics committee decision is filed *as part of* the TİTCK application, so the two cannot be run in parallel to save calendar time. Plan the lead time accordingly (`05`).
   - **m.7/1(b): 20 iş günü** for TİTCK review of a **complete** file — the clock starts on completeness, not on submission.
   - **m.5/9: insurance is mandatory.**
   - **m.5/6: the site must qualify.** A general private Acıbadem hospital does **not**; the study must sit at **Acıbadem Mehmet Ali Aydınlar Üniversitesi's uygulama ve araştırma merkezi**. Open on the product owner as `00 §4` B5.
   - **m.11: an amendment is a fresh submission**, which is why the runtime gate **fails closed on protocol amendment** (`10 §9.1` inv. 23).

   Consequences that are binding on engineering, not aspirational: output goes to a **sealed research store** and to the investigator per protocol — **never** to a treating clinician at session time, **never** into the pre-visit note, **never** into an `Observation`. The former plan to store it as an `Observation` with `dataAbsentReason` "until validated" is **deleted** (`03 §9`): writing it into the clinical record *is* putting the function into service, and the `dataAbsentReason` wrapper changes nothing about that. Outside the investigation the module is **genuinely unreachable, not merely unrendered** — suppressing an output is not the same as not having the function, and running it anyway would additionally process special-category data for a purpose that benefits no one, least of all the patient. **Unlike triage, `acoustic` has no shadow mode.**

## 2. Consent architecture (unchanged mechanics, now bound to law)
Purposes exactly: `core`, `voice`, `wellbeing`, `lane:affect`, `lane:linguistic`, `documents`, `learning:personal` (on by default, inspectable — legal basis: performance of the very service the patient configured), `learning:service_improvement` (de-identified aggregates; opt-out costs no feature), `satisfaction`, `share:{recipient,visit,sections,expiry}`, `research`. Each consent stores `{version, text_hash, ts, channel, evidence}`; texts live only in `docs/legal/` (INT-210) and the UI renders from source so what was shown is provable. Explicit-consent quality rules are executable tests: no pre-ticked boxes, no bundling, no service-conditioning, withdrawal as easy as grant, cascade ≤ 1 min (INT-038, INT-132).

> ⚠ **`lane:acoustic` is removed from this list, 25 Aug 2026.** It was a consent purpose; it is now an **investigational-device informed-consent form** (§2.4). A device you may not place on the market is not made lawful by a toggle, and offering it as one in a commercial tenant would be offering the patient a choice we have no right to offer.

### 2.1 What every lane text must say
Turkish-first, PolicyGuard-protected, **unbundled and independently refusable with no penalty beyond the lane itself.** Every lane consent states five things, in this order: the **purpose**; the **vendor by legal name and country**; **whether audio or only text leaves Türkiye**; **retention**; and **how to withdraw**.

- **`voice`** names **exactly** the vendors that receive audio — after the one-stream fix (`03 §4.7.2`), **one**. `consent_vendor_parity` (`10 §9.1` inv. 25) fails CI if the names here and the egress allow-list hosts tagged `receives:audio` ever diverge, so the text cannot quietly become false again.
- **`lane:affect`** must say that the system **infers emotional state from the sound of the voice, not only from the words** — a patient who thinks we analyse only what they said has not understood what they agreed to. It must say that this is **not a diagnosis and is not shared with the doctor as an assessment**, and that it only adapts pace and format. Under the Option-B default the text can also say the thing that is worth more than the engineering delta: *"bu analiz Türkiye'deki sunucularımızda yapılır; ek ses aktarımı olmaz."*
- **`lane:linguistic`** is text-only, and **must carry the crisis carve-out in its own text**: certain phrases about self-harm trigger a fixed safety response **regardless of this choice** (`04 §4.2` defect 3). The honest place to disclose an exception is the consent that does not cover it — not a paragraph elsewhere in a policy document.

### 2.2 Withdrawal mid-session — effective ≤ 1 s at the lane boundary
Mechanically: the lane task is cancelled; buffers are **zeroized and the zeroization is asserted** (`10 §3`); if the lane rode a shared stream, the vendor request is reconfigured or the socket is **torn down and re-established without profiling** (~200–400 ms STT gap — acceptable, and **tested rather than discovered in production**); live `ObserverState` entries are dropped; already-emitted `LaneSignal` events are **tombstoned, not deleted** — deleting one breaks the audit hash chain — and are non-consumable on replay (`03 §4.8`).

**Engine decisions already taken are not retroactively undone.** You cannot un-ask a question. They are instead **annotated in the note** as derived from a since-withdrawn signal. **This is the point an auditor will press on, so it is decided here rather than at the audit**: silently reversing a decision would falsify the record of what actually happened to the patient, and silently keeping it would misrepresent the consent basis. Annotation is the only option that is true. Property-tested at random withdrawal points by `consent_withdrawal_guard` (inv. 24).

### 2.3 Withdrawal after the fact
Delete lane-derived aggregates and the **"Konuşma özellikleri"** block from the note and from any delivered Doctor Link (revoke and reissue), cascade ≤ 1 min. **Where a note has already been pushed to a hospital HBYS/FHIR endpoint, deletion is the hospital's as controller (§0.1) — say so plainly to the patient rather than promising a deletion we cannot perform**, and emit a FHIR `Consent`/`Provenance` update so the hospital's own record shows the withdrawal. A promise we cannot keep is a worse answer than an honest boundary, and it is also the one that fails a DSAR audit.

### 2.4 `lane:acoustic` → an investigational-device ICF, not a toggle
Under §0.7 the acoustic module runs only inside the registered investigation, so its patient-facing artefact is an **informed consent form**, naming: the **study ID and protocol version**; the **investigator**; the **KAEK approval number** and the **TİTCK permission**; the **insurance** (TCKAY m.5/9); that **a voice-based depression and anxiety score is computed**; that it is **not validated in Turkish**; that it **will not affect their care**; **who sees it**; that **audio is retained** for the study **and for how long**; and the **right to withdraw at any time without any effect on their care**. Consent is documented per TCKAY, not stored as a `{version, text_hash}` row alongside the product purposes. **An ICF, not a toggle** — the difference is not cosmetic, because the ICF is the artefact a regulator asks for and a toggle is the artefact that proves we treated a device as a feature.

## 3. Retention matrix (enforced by INT-036; policy doc INT-212)

## 10. AI Act exposure — out of territorial scope, written to anyway

**Territorially, not currently in scope.** Art. 2 reaches providers **placing systems on the EU market** and third-country providers or deployers **whose output is used in the Union**. Turkish patients, Turkish clinicians and Turkish output fall outside all of those limbs. This changes the day we CE-mark for the EU, and it changes at that moment — not gradually.

**If in scope, `affect` is squarely an emotion recognition system.** Art. 3(39): *"identifying or inferring emotions or intentions of natural persons on the basis of their biometric data."* That is the lane's description, not a strained reading of it. **Annex III point 1(c) then makes it high-risk.**

**The trap worth writing down: this bites regardless of MDR class.** Annex III attaches to **the AI system as such** — it is an independent route to high-risk, parallel to Art. 6(1)'s medical-device route. So the M1 non-device argument under MDR (§0.6), however well it holds, does **nothing** for AI Act status. Two regimes, two tests, one system; passing one is not evidence about the other.

**Art. 5(1)(f) does not apply.** The prohibition on emotion recognition is limited to **workplace and education institutions**. A clinic intake is not the data subject's workplace. (It is the *clinician's* workplace, which is not the same question and not the one Art. 5 asks.)

**Timing, and the part that is easy to get backwards.** Reg. (EU) **2026/1744** (the Digital Omnibus on AI), OJ **24 July 2026**, in force **27 July 2026**, deferred the **Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027**. But **Art. 50(3) transparency was *not* deferred** and applies from **2 August 2026** (`00 §2`). Reading "the AI Act was postponed" as covering transparency is the error to avoid.

**Practical instruction, therefore: although out of territorial scope today, write the `lane:affect` consent to the Art. 50(3) standard now** (§2.1). It costs one sentence. It is the difference between a CE application that already conforms and one that reopens **every consent artefact** — texts, hashes, acceptance records, the UI that renders them — at the worst possible moment in the programme. For the MDR/AIA interplay generally, point the file at **MDCG 2025-6**.

**Türkiye: no AI-specific statute is in force.** Three TBMM proposals (one 2024, two 2025), **none enacted**. The **AI Action Plan 2026–2030** entered force via **Cumhurbaşkanlığı Genelgesi 2026/9, RG 18 Aug 2026** — a policy instrument, not a source of obligations. Regulation of this product therefore runs through **KVKK, TCK and 5651**, which are already mapped in §1. Do not tell a partner or an investor that Türkiye "has an AI law".
