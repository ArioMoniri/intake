# 06 · Legal & regulatory frame — firm obligations, firm positioning, firm implementation map (v1.4, 21 Aug 2026)

> This file is written to be **relied on**, not hedged: each obligation cites the instrument (Resmî Gazete date/no where verified in this research pass), states what INTAKE must do, and names the backlog issue(s) that implement it (codes resolve in `11_ISSUE_BACKLOG.md`). Items marked **[counsel]** are the small set where a Turkish health-tech lawyer must confirm interpretation before the related `approval:human-legal` PR merges — the engineering is built either way. Nothing here is optional and nothing is deferred without a written counsel opinion in `docs/legal/opinions/`.

## 0. Firm legal positioning of the product
1. **Two data-protection roles, by deployment.** (a) *Consumer app (patient-owned record)*: INTAKE (the company) is **veri sorumlusu / controller** (KVKK m.3). (b) *Hospital deployment (clinic console, kiosk, connectors)*: the hospital is controller; INTAKE is **veri işleyen / processor** under a written DPA — the same role the SBYS regulation assigns to hospital-software vendors. Every table, screen and export knows which role applies (tenant flag; INT-030, INT-250).
2. **Not a remote-health service.** INTAKE collects and structures patient-reported information *before* a visit and delivers it to the clinician; no health professional provides examination, consultation or treatment *through* INTAKE. Therefore the Uzaktan Sağlık Hizmetlerinin Sunumu Hakkında Yönetmelik (RG 31746, 10.02.2022) does not govern the intake itself. The moment any tenant wants clinician↔patient interaction through us (the `teleconsult` visit type merely *prepares* for the hospital's own tele-visit), that feature requires the facility's MoH uzaktan-sağlık permit and a **registered USBS** (Yönetmelik m.5, m.14(a)); we do not build it before USBS tescili — enforced as a feature gate, not a promise.
3. **Medical-device boundary is explicit and flag-enforced.** Under the Tıbbi Cihaz Yönetmeliği (RG 31499 mükerrer, 02.06.2021 — full MDR 2017/745 alignment) and MDCG 2019-11 (TİTCK's own Turkish translation), two functions are **MDSW**: automated red-flag triage that directs a patient's urgency, and automated interpretation of screening instruments. Classification: Annex VIII **Rule 11 → Class IIb for both device functions**, with a Class III argument that must be rebutted in writing — rationale documented, notified body to confirm (INT-216) **[counsel]**. *(Corrected 25 Aug 2026. The previous "Class IIa" position is not defensible for three reasons. (1) IMDRF N12 — the framework MDCG 2019-11 Annex III explicitly adopts — defines "drive clinical management" as including "**triage** early signs of a disease or condition". Critical situation x drive = IMDRF III.i = **Class IIb**. (2) MDCG says to assess the impact "**if based on incorrect information from the MDSW**". The foreseeable incorrect output is a **false negative** — a missed MI or stroke, so the patient does not call 112 and presents outside the thrombolysis/PCI window. That is the death/irreversible limb, i.e. the **Class III** argument. The former rationale assessed false positives only, which is not the test. (3) Decisively, **MDCG 2019-11 Annex IV contains our second device function almost verbatim**: "Diagnostic MDSW intended for scoring depression based on inputted data on a patient's symptoms (e.g. mood, anxiety) should be classified as **class IIb** under Rule 11(a) (IMDRF Risk Category III.ii)". Instrument interpretation is therefore not arguable. Note also MDCG's nearest analogue for the triage function: a heartbeat app that detects abnormalities and **informs a physician** is IIb — our output instructs a **lay patient** to call an ambulance with no clinician in between, which cannot be a lower class. IIa would be reachable only with an intended purpose materially narrower than §0.3 — no urgency direction, no condition-specific red flags, emergency instruction as a static non-algorithmic notice — which is precisely `device_functions=off`. **Stage A is the coherent half of the plan; the former Stage B IIa target was the incoherent half.** Consequences to re-plan on: Annex IX Ch. II technical-documentation assessment per generic device group rather than IIa sampling; clinical investigation generally expected rather than literature-based equivalence; **PSUR annually (IIb) rather than >= every 2 years (IIa)**; longer notified-body queue and higher cost. **Buy a written NB classification opinion before INT-217's scope and budget are fixed** — a EUR 5-15k question guarding a seven-figure programme, and a IIa->IIb surprise at classification review is a ~9-12 month schedule break. Implementing rule 3.5 requires the strictest applicable rule, so INT-216 must carry **one classification rationale per device function**, not one for the product.)* Everything else (structured intake, summaries, record organisation, Doctor Link transport) is non-device documentation/communication software per MDCG 2019-11.
   **Firm two-stage plan:** *Stage A (P0–P1):* commercial tenants run with `device_functions=off` — no automated patient-facing urgency advice, no automated severity labels; red-flag answers are displayed to clinicians as patient-reported data; fixed "Acil bir durumda 112'yi arayın" text appears at static points; the deterministic triage runs in **shadow** to build clinical-evaluation evidence. The Acıbadem pilot runs `device_functions=on` **only** as a registered **clinical investigation**: ATADEK ethics approval + TİTCK permission under the Tıbbi Cihaz Klinik Araştırmaları Yönetmeliği and 3359 s. Kanun Ek m.10 (approval and permission are required for *all* device studies, observational included), with insurance as required and the study ID bound to the tenant flag (INT-117, INT-218). *Stage B (P2–P3):* ISO 13485 QMS, IEC 62304 (class B) lifecycle, ISO 14971 risk file, IEC 62366-1 usability, IEC 81001-5-1 security lifecycle, clinical evaluation per MDCG 2020-1, notified-body conformity assessment, PRRC appointment (m.15), UDI + **ÜTS** registration under TİTCK's TCKKD-KLVZ-03 guide (in force 13.05.2026) — then `device_functions=on` commercially (INT-217/219/220).
4. **No diagnosis, ever.** 1219 sayılı Kanun m.1/m.8 reserves diagnosis and treatment to licensed physicians; every note and summary carries the fixed sentence *"Bu belge hastanın beyanına dayanır; hekim değerlendirmesinin yerine geçmez."* and the app never names a suspected diagnosis to the patient (PolicyGuard rule + INT-084 golden).
5. **Learning under change control.** Because triage-adjacent content is (or will be) device content, Loop 2 proposals touching device functions go through QMS design-change control in addition to clinician review; the proposal pipeline is technically unable to modify red-flag levels, instruments or consent texts (INT-172; `09 §4`).

## 2. Consent architecture (unchanged mechanics, now bound to law)
Purposes exactly: `core`, `voice`, `wellbeing`, `lane:affect`, `lane:linguistic`, `lane:acoustic` (research tenants only), `documents`, `learning:personal` (on by default, inspectable — legal basis: performance of the very service the patient configured), `learning:service_improvement` (de-identified aggregates; opt-out costs no feature), `satisfaction`, `share:{recipient,visit,sections,expiry}`, `research`. Each consent stores `{version, text_hash, ts, channel, evidence}`; texts live only in `docs/legal/` (INT-210) and the UI renders from source so what was shown is provable. Explicit-consent quality rules are executable tests: no pre-ticked boxes, no bundling, no service-conditioning, withdrawal as easy as grant, cascade ≤ 1 min (INT-038, INT-132).

## 3. Retention matrix (enforced by INT-036; policy doc INT-212)
Raw audio: **never written** (guard test). Lane windows: memory-only, zeroed after scoring. Transcript-derived structured data & notes: life of the record, patient-deletable (30-day grace, then crypto-shred). Doctor Link tokens: expiry ≤ 72 h default. Consent & audit records: 10 years (hospital tenants) — audit is WORM. Telemetry: 30 d hot / 1 y cold, PHI-free by construction. 5651 traffic logs: 1 y (max 2), segregated. Satisfaction: with account. Research extracts: per ethics protocol. Destruction runs ≤ every 6 months with logged reports.

## 4. Security controls = the Board's adequate measures (canonical in `10`; mapping in INT-215)
Everything in `10 §2–§8` exists to satisfy KVKK m.12 + Karar 2018/10 + KSVY + DDO Rehberi at once: envelope encryption with per-tenant keys, tokenised identifiers, mesh mTLS, MFA/passkeys for staff and 2FA-equivalent (attestation+biometric+DPoP) for patient remote access to special-category data, WAF + egress allow-list (the transfer register's evidence), de-identification before any cloud model call, WORM audit with daily verification, immutable encrypted backups with drills, SIEM/runtime detection, 72-h breach machinery, quarterly access reviews, annual pen-test. A failing compliance control pages like a failing SLO (`08 §5`).

## 6. Minors, companions, kiosk — firm rules (INT-223, INT-133, INT-143)
Under-18: guardian account holds the legal consents (TMK velâyet; HHY-24); the adolescent (12–17) additionally acknowledges in-app and the adolescent-private section is delivered to the clinician; guardian visibility of that section follows the treating clinic's written policy within HHY information rights — the app records which policy applied. Companion/proxy answers are always flagged `proxy_reported` on the record. Kiosk sessions are ephemeral by construction (60-s wipe) so no bystander data persists.

## 8. Learning loops, admin console, CI/CD — legal notes (as before, now firm)
Loop 1 = service performance inside the patient's record; Loop 2 = de-identified service-improvement aggregates (opt-out, k ≥ 10); anything publishable or voice-model-training = `research` + ethics + KSVY-16 anonymisation. Device-relevant proposals additionally pass QMS change control (§0.5). Console: no PHI by default, break-glass 4-eyes, tenant isolation; hospital tenants never see each other. CI: no PHI, synthetic fixtures, TR-DC runners; deploy approvals audited.

## 8b. Technical monitoring as a compliance control
Retention runs, consent denials, egress-vs-register diffs, audit-chain verification, backup freshness, drill and access-review recency, DSAR SLA clocks, SCC-notification due dates, ÜTS/KTS/VERBİS status are catalogued metrics with alerts (`08 §4 K/M`); a red compliance signal pages on-call.
